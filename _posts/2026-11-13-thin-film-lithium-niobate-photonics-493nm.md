Visible and near-ultraviolet light underpins applications from biophotonics to quantum computing that demand fast control of optical amplitude and phase, yet most visible-wavelength integrated photonic platforms lack gigahertz-scale modulation. The authors demonstrate a thin-film lithium niobate platform operating at 493 nanometers that integrates grating couplers, beamsplitters, ring resonators, and modulators. Their segmented traveling-wave modulators achieve a half-wave voltage-length product of 450 millivolt-centimeters, more than 20 decibels of extinction, and bandwidth beyond 50 gigahertz. Ring resonators show propagation loss of 2.9 plus or minus 0.2 decibels per centimeter and are used to quantify the platform's photorefractive response. The results enable agile visible beam delivery for systems such as trapped-ion quantum computers, although the authors note that photorefraction and substrate uniformity remain key challenges to scaling the platform further.
