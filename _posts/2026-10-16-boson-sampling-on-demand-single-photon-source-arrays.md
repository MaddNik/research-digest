Boson sampling using arrays of on-demand single photons, that is discrete-variable Fock states, offers complexity-scaling advantages over resource states mimicked by continuous-variable sources from nonlinear photonics, and recent progress in regular arrays of mesa top single quantum dots has opened a path to chip-scale boson sampling with single photons. The authors build a framework that directly links physical sources of decoherence to the outcome of multiphoton correlation measurements, explicitly incorporating the symmetrization of N-photon states while accounting for individual wave packet shapes and dephasing. Applying this framework, they provide predictive estimates for generating a four-photon NOON state using an optical circuit initialized with four identical single-photon wave packets that mimic photons emitted from mesa top single quantum dot sources reported in prior work. The results show how the probability of generating various four-photon entangled states depends on two-photon interference visibility, which is set by the ratio of pure dephasing timescale to radiative decay timescale. The framework provides quantitative guidelines for measuring four-photon and higher-order entangled states in optical circuits built from on-demand single-photon sources, linking physical processes such as temperature to circuit output states.
