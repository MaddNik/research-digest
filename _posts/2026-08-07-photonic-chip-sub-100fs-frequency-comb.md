Sub-100-femtosecond pulse and frequency comb sources are normally generated by tabletop mode-locked lasers, which are complex, environmentally sensitive, and hard to maintain over the long term, motivating chip-scale alternatives. The authors combine two photonic chip technologies: thin-film lithium niobate electro-optic modulators to generate picosecond seed pulses, and highly nonlinear optical loop mirrors based on AlGaAs-on-insulator nanowaveguides for temporal pulse cleaning and spectral broadening. Starting from a seed pulse centered near 1550 nanometers, a single-stage AlGaAs nonlinear optical loop mirror with a 1 centimeter loop length produces nearly tenfold spectral broadening, more than tenfold pulse compression, and over 10 decibels of pulse pedestal suppression. Using initial electro-optic comb pulses at repetition rates of 10 to 20 gigahertz, the authors demonstrate output pulses as short as 55 femtoseconds with a flat-topped comb spectrum whose 10 decibel optical bandwidth exceeds 90 nanometers. The authors present this as a route toward miniaturized, high-repetition-rate sub-100-femtosecond optical pulse generators with potentially improved stability and operability compared to tabletop systems.
