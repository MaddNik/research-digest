---

Distributed quantum computing needs rapid, high-fidelity entanglement between qubits held in separate processor modules, and photonic links offer switchable long-range connections but are limited by photon loss. The authors entangle a trapped barium-138 ion with a silicon-vacancy center in a diamond nanophotonic cavity by converting a photon emitted by the ion in wavelength and encoding, reflecting it from the silicon-vacancy-cavity system, and detecting it so that a single detected photon heralds entanglement. Including all conversion losses, they generate ion-silicon-vacancy Bell pairs at an average rate of 1.03 kilohertz with a fidelity of 87.9 percent. This is four times the rate of the fastest previous photonic link between two trapped ions and corresponds to about one Bell pair per millisecond, matching the cycle time expected for planned trapped-ion processors.
