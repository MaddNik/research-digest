Conventional free-space optical neural networks are cascades of linear optical components followed by square-law detection, which restricts each output score to a quadratic form with a rank-one decision matrix. This paper presents a random-mapped intensity optical neural network, an all-optical two-layer computational network that lifts this rank-one limit by combining a high-dimensional feature projection from a disordered medium, a programmable nonnegative intensity mask, and segmented spatial power summation. The authors numerically show this combination can surpass the rank-one ceiling through higher-rank quadratic decision boundaries, and they exploit the vectorial coherent wave-mixing nature of the disordered medium to experimentally validate multimodal classification of amplitude, phase, and polarization on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, and Quick Draw using a single optical configuration across all encoding domains. The results provide a practical basis for scalable direct-field optical processors that can exploit amplitude, phase, and polarization information within one unified intensity-based inference framework.
