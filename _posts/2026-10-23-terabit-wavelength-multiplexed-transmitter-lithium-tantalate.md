Artificial-intelligence systems face a growing data-movement bottleneck that demands higher interconnect bandwidth without proportionally more electrical power, laser sources, or fiber connections. Wavelength multiplexing eases the fiber budget, but integrating shared multi-wavelength sources with dense wavelength routing and broadband modulation in a compact format has remained difficult. The authors demonstrate a monolithic transmitter, enabled by the low birefringence and native Pockels response of thin-film lithium tantalate, that separates, independently modulates, and recombines eight wavelength channels between a single optical input and output while keeping a two-fiber interface as the channel count grows. Measurements across the eight channels establish an aggregate line rate of 1.6 terabits per second, and operation with a shared optical frequency comb achieves an estimated net data rate of 0.58 terabits per second across four channels, showing compatibility with a common multi-wavelength source. The authors argue this architecture scales toward 10 terabit-per-second optical interfaces with high bandwidth per unit package edge, positioning thin-film lithium tantalate for the next generation of co-packaged optical interconnects.
