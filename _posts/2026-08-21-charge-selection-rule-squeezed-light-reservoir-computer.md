---

This theoretical paper analyzes optical quantum reservoir computers that encode data in the phase of a parametric pump, focusing on what such systems can compute and the measurement cost of reading out results. The author identifies a conservation law, described as pairwise photon exchange conserving an integer phase charge, which determines that order-D readout can only reach combinations of at most D unit-charge kernels across an ensemble of input masks. The paper states that degree-one homodyne readout is universal for a class of fading-memory functionals along weak-squeezing families, at a shot cost that grows polynomially with accuracy, while at fixed squeezing no finite readout degree can reach every sector of possible functions. The author frames nonlinearity as residing in the optics rather than the detector, since per-shot measurement variance is fixed at every readout order. Using a hardware-faithful digital twin rather than a physical device, the author reports that a displacement-encoded control scheme at identical photon number loses 17.7 accuracy points on an open radio-frequency dataset, consistent with predictions from the charge conservation argument.
