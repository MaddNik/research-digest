This review covers recent advances in integrated photonic quantum chips as a platform for fault-tolerant photonic quantum computers, comparing them with bulk-optics systems on compactness, programmability, and stability. The authors survey both discrete-variable and continuous-variable approaches to universal photonic quantum computation, summarizing progress in fault-tolerant architectures and state-of-the-art integrated platforms. They highlight the key challenges that remain before photonic systems can scale to full fault tolerance. The paper closes by outlining future research directions meant to bridge the gap between today's proof-of-principle demonstrations and fully scalable quantum microprocessors built on photonic chips.
