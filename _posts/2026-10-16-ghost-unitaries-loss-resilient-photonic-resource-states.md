description: "A theoretical study shows that applying resource-generation interferometers to the heralding modes rather than the output modes can make probabilistic photonic entangled-state generation more resilient to loss."
---

Entangled few-photon states such as Bell and GHZ states are central resources for discrete-variable photonic quantum information processing, and are typically generated by heralding single photons from biphoton sources and interfering them in linear-optical circuits. This work shows that the entanglement structure of those biphoton sources can be exploited directly for noise engineering, introducing "ghost-unitary" implementations in which the target resource-generation interferometer acts on the heralding modes instead of the delivered resource modes. When interferometric loss dominates, this relocation converts direct amplitude damping of the output into false-positive heralding events that require both higher-pair emission and photon loss, which are parametrically suppressed in the low-squeezing regime and yield higher-fidelity conditional resource states. The authors analyze this mechanism for single-qubit states, two-photon NOON states, Bell states, a family of n-qubit GHZ states, and single-qubit teleportation, identifying finite parameter regimes where ghost-unitary implementations outperform direct ones, especially for relatively lossy frequency-bin photonics. They also prove a structural result showing that direct implementations of the n-GHZ family produce outputs equivalent to the ideal state followed by uniform amplitude damping, extending previously known cases.
