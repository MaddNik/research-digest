Researchers demonstrate a fully integrated silicon quantum photon-pair source that operates without active tuning of any component, addressing a major power-consumption bottleneck for scaling complex silicon quantum photonic circuits. The circuit combines photon-pair generation in a micro-ring resonator, pump rejection using Bragg filters, and signal and idler demultiplexing through modal add-drop filters, with building blocks engineered to minimize sensitivity to fabrication variations. It achieves coincidence rates up to 4000 counts per second with coincidence-to-accidental ratios as high as 100 across the generated spectrum. Separate two-photon interference measurements yield raw visibilities exceeding 93 percent for individually selected ITU wavelength-channel pairs. By eliminating the need for active spectral tuning while maintaining high quantum performance, the work points toward more scalable silicon quantum photonic systems.
