Photon loss is a key limitation for fault-tolerant photonic quantum computing based on Gottesman-Kitaev-Preskill, or GKP, bosonic codes. The authors model the pure-loss channel directly and estimate photon-loss thresholds for the surface-GKP code using nearest-lattice-point inner GKP decoding combined with minimum-weight perfect matching for the outer surface code. For GKP states with squeezing of 20 decibels and 13 decibels, the baseline thresholds are 5.5 percent and 5.9 percent loss respectively. Introducing a new anti-photon-loss inner decoder that compensates for the loss-induced contraction of the measured quadrature raises these thresholds to 9.8 percent and 6.3 percent. The results show that accounting for loss-induced lattice contraction in the inner decoder meaningfully improves the photon-loss tolerance of surface-GKP codes for both high-quality and moderate-quality GKP states.
