Researchers report the entanglement-based segment of the Slovak national quantum communication infrastructure, connecting four cities in western Slovakia over deployed telecommunication fiber. Polarization-entangled photons generated at a central node are passively routed by wavelength to form a trusted-node-free, fully meshed sub-network of three cities, with a fourth city connected through the central node, all links operating simultaneously. Unlike most previous demonstrations that estimate key rates from raw coincidences, the rates reported here come from the complete post-processing chain, with keys delivered in real time via the ETSI GS QKD 014 interface. With partial non-local chromatic dispersion compensation, the team obtains 13.5 bits per second over 109 kilometers, and 4.3 bits per second over 32.1 decibels of accumulated loss between two cities without a direct fiber link. Benchmarking against finite-key security bounds shows that the implemented heuristic privacy amplification can exceed the finite-key bound, and that replacing detect-and-discard with error correction would increase the finite-key rate by a factor of 4.1 to 6.6, yielding a positive finite-key rate on a 142.9 kilometer link where the implemented scheme currently yields none.
