Directly modulated vertical-cavity surface-emitting lasers are central to short-reach optical interconnects, but conventional oxide VCSELs typically need power-hungry signal processing to reach data rates above 100 gigabits per second. This paper demonstrates transverse detuned loading, in which carrier-induced index variations reshape an engineered transverse mode so that its overlap with spatially selective optical losses decreases as carrier density rises, producing a negative differential loss analogous to detuned-loaded distributed Bragg reflector lasers. The resulting single-mode 850 nanometer VCSEL shows an enhanced carrier-photon relaxation frequency of 44 gigahertz and a 3 decibel electrical modulation bandwidth exceeding 50 gigahertz, with the enhancement persisting from 25 to 75 degrees Celsius. The same device supports 106.25 gigabits per second non-return-to-zero transmission without digital signal processing, transmitter pre-emphasis, or receiver equalization, establishing transverse detuned loading as a robust alternative for extending VCSEL modulation bandwidth.
