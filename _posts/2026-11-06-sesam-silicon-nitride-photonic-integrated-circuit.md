Researchers demonstrate hybrid integration of a semiconductor saturable absorber mirror, known as a SESAM, onto a silicon nitride photonic integrated circuit using a micro-assembly process. The approach bonds the SESAM to inverse-tapered silicon nitride waveguides, transferring the absorber's nonlinear saturable absorption behavior onto the photonic chip. At a wavelength of 1560 nanometers, the bonded device achieves a waveguide-coupled modulation depth of 5.2 percent, a maximum reflectivity of 37.2 percent, and a saturation fluence of 47 microjoules per square centimeter. The authors characterize both the unbonded SESAM and the bonded, waveguide-coupled version to confirm that the nonlinear functionality survives the integration process. This work establishes a path toward fully integrated, self-starting, chip-scale femtosecond lasers built on silicon nitride photonic integrated circuit platforms.
