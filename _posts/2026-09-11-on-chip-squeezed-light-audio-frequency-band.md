Audio-frequency squeezed light is a key resource for quantum metrology and sensing, but generating it stably on integrated photonic platforms has been hindered by technical noise and the difficulty of scalable phase referencing. The authors demonstrate on-chip generation of audio-band two-mode squeezed states down to 60 hertz in a silica microcavity. They develop a coherent-comb control method in which a weak electro-optic reference comb co-propagates with the vacuum at the quantum frequency modes in an orthogonal polarization, providing quadrature measurement and long-timescale phase stability without directly locking the fragile quantum modes. Using the positive partial transposition criterion, they verify entanglement with a minimum symplectic eigenvalue of 0.395, confirming inseparability, and establish a scalable route toward phase-stable audio-band squeezing for continuous-variable quantum information processing.
