Heterogeneous integration combines complementary material properties within a single photonic platform. This paper demonstrates on-chip f-2f self-referencing in a heterogeneously integrated silicon nitride and lithium niobate platform, where supercontinuum generation happens in the silicon nitride waveguide and second-harmonic generation happens in the lithium niobate layer, spatially separated and linked by adiabatic escalator couplers. Pumped by a 1560 nanometer mode-locked laser with 75 picojoules of on-chip pulse energy, the authors detect the carrier-envelope offset frequency with a 30 decibel signal-to-noise ratio in a 300 kilohertz resolution bandwidth. A comparative measurement confirms the second harmonic originates in the lithium niobate layer, and the authors note that periodic poling could offer further improvements. The results establish heterogeneous silicon nitride and lithium niobate integration as a viable building block for self-referenced ultrafast pulse sources in low-loss silicon nitride photonics.
