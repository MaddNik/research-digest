Most optical neural networks encode information across space, either in free-space arrays or integrated waveguide meshes, which ties the achievable number of neurons to the number of physical components and fixes the routing topology at fabrication time. This paper shows that moving the computation into the time domain instead, using thin-film lithium niobate modulators as time lenses and temporal masks with chromatic dispersion providing coupling between successive temporal neurons, decouples the computational dimension from the hardware dimension. The authors experimentally verify high-order, complex-valued matrix-matrix multiplications through a single optical input and output port, and by adding optical feedback they build fully programmable, in-situ trainable temporal diffractive neural networks. These networks are validated on classification benchmarks as well as image and video generation tasks, including an all-analogue generative pipeline whose latent variable is drawn directly from amplified spontaneous emission with no digital sampling anywhere in the generative path. The authors report high-resolution image and video generation at high frame rates, outperforming prior modulator-refresh-limited optical generative systems.
