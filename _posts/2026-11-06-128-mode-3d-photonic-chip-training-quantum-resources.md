This paper addresses how to program three-dimensional, continuously coupled waveguide-array photonic chips, a device class that can scale to large numbers of optical modes but lacks known analytical methods for setting their parameters. The authors use a machine learning algorithm driven by quantum data to control a reconfigurable photonic device with 128 modes. Using only a limited number of one-photon and two-photon measurements, the method experimentally reaches target unitary matrices among those achievable by the device. The approach remains effective in the presence of noise and compensates partially for imperfections in the experimental setup. The result suggests a practical route for programming large-scale integrated photonic devices for which no simple analytical decomposition exists.
