description: "A new reachability criterion for coupled-waveguide photon sources screens materials and modes that can produce spectrally pure photon pairs by spontaneous parametric down-conversion without needing periodic poling."
---

Producing spectrally pure photon pairs by spontaneous parametric down-conversion without periodic poling would broaden the choice of usable nonlinear materials, but it requires optical modes that provide both phase matching and suitable group velocities. The authors derive a joint reachability criterion for a constant-cross-section coupler with a nonlinear main guide and a linear auxiliary waveguide. In a two-mode description, a phase-closure condition fixes the hybridization parameter, which also determines the main-guide participation and the attainable group-delay ratio, letting the criterion screen candidate material and mode pairs and specify detuning and participation targets for more detailed modeling. A first-order purity map connects the reachable delay ratio and bandwidth-length product to the resulting photon-pair spectrum. A full-vector example using an aluminum nitride and titanium dioxide waveguide pair satisfies direct phase matching with a group-delay mismatch ratio of negative 0.899 and yields a calculated unfiltered Schmidt purity of 0.789 over 5 millimeters, illustrating how the criterion links phase closure, dispersion control, and nonlinear participation for coupled-waveguide photon sources beyond periodically poled materials.
