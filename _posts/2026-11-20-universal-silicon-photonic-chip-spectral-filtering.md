The authors demonstrate a universal silicon photonic chip that combines two 8 by 8 programmable unitary transformation circuits connected by optical intensity modulators, forming a single universal linear optical circuit. By deliberately using different optical path lengths between the two unitary circuits instead of equal-length interconnections, the chip gains wavelength-dependent phase diversity while keeping the circuit fully programmable. The fabricated device reaches a spectral resolution of 0.05 nanometers over more than 100 nanometers of bandwidth, measures eight independent laser sources at once, and produces multiple programmable spectral filter functions simultaneously. The authors also show the chip performing spectral measurement and filtering together while the filter functions are being reconfigured dynamically. The work positions universal photonic circuits as a general platform for combining spectral sensing with programmable optical signal processing on one chip.
