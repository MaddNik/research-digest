The authors study InAs quantum dots embedded in relaxed InGaAs matrices grown on metamorphic InGaAs layers, a platform intended to push quantum dot emission into the telecommunication O- and C-bands for fiber-based quantum communication. By comparing coherent quasi-resonant and phonon-assisted excitation schemes, they separate excitation-induced effects from the dots' intrinsic optical properties. The measurements reveal linewidths as narrow as 21 micro-electronvolts, the disappearance of slow decay channels under certain excitation conditions, and single-photon purity with second-order autocorrelation values around 10 to the negative 2. Photoluminescence excitation spectroscopy further reveals higher excited states, enabling coherent control of trion transitions and the observation of Rabi-like oscillations in both telecom bands, supporting the potential of these quantum dots for fiber-based quantum technologies.
