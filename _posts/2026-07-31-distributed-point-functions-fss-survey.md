A distributed point function is a cryptographic primitive enabling compressed additive secret sharing of a point function, a vector that is zero everywhere except one secret position, and its lightweight structure has enabled applications including private information retrieval, anonymous messaging, secure computation with preprocessing, and pseudorandom correlation generators that expand small correlated seeds into large correlated randomness. This article surveys the definitions, constructions, and applications of distributed point functions and discusses their extension to function secret sharing, which generalizes point functions to richer function classes while preserving most of the same application benefits. This is a survey rather than a paper presenting new results, but it is included given its authorship by the primitive's original co-inventors and its direct relevance to multi-party-computation-adjacent primitives.
