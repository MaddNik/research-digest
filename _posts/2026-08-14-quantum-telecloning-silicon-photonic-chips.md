Quantum telecloning combines teleportation with cloning to distribute copies of an unknown quantum state to multiple distant parties. The authors implement this using a six-photon protocol split between two silicon photonic chips connected by optical fiber, where one chip generates the input state while the other prepares an entangled resource state. An interchip Bell-state measurement then produces two optimal clones at remote locations, achieving a fidelity of 78.45 plus or minus 1.39 percent, exceeding the classical cloning threshold by 8 standard deviations. The result demonstrates robust generation and manipulation of complex multi-photon states shared between separate integrated chips. The authors present this as infrastructure relevant to scaling multi-party quantum networks.
