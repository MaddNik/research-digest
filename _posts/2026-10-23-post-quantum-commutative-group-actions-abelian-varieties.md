The authors study the cryptographic use of Shimura class group actions on superspecial principally polarized abelian varieties equipped with a CM orientation, situating the construction within post-quantum isogeny-based cryptography. Beyond giving an accessible introduction illustrated with examples, they provide tools for representing CM orientations through higher-dimensional isogenies and for computing the Shimura class group action in the restricted effective group action model. Key technical ingredients include a version of Kani's lemma adapted to the CM setting and a technique that often allows halving the ambient dimension, along with a method for finding superspecial principally polarized abelian surfaces oriented by a given CM order, drawn from an algebraic framework built on the Ibukiyama-Katsura-Oort correspondence. The paper also analyzes obstructions to transitivity of the Shimura class group action related to the characteristic-zero notion of CM type, contributing foundational tools for isogeny-based key exchange in higher dimensions.
