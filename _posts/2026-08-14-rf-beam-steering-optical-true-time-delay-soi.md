The authors present the design, packaging, and system-level demonstration of a reconfigurable 5-bit, 4-channel optical true time delay chip built on a 3 micrometer thick silicon-on-insulator platform. The chip provides 32 discrete delay states spanning 0 to 101 picoseconds with 3.25 picosecond resolution, targeting broadband RF beamforming for upcoming 6G FR3-band systems near 10 gigahertz. Each channel uses five binary-weighted delay stages controlled by Mach-Zehnder interferometer thermo-optic switches with an extinction ratio above 25 decibels and average switching power of 25 milliwatts. Embedded in an analog radio-over-fiber link driving a four-element patch antenna array, the system demonstrated accurate beam steering at broadside and at 30 degrees off broadside using a 16-QAM signal at 100 megabaud on a 10 gigahertz carrier, with only about 1 percent error vector magnitude degradation upon steering. The authors present this as evidence that integrated optical true time delay technology is ready for compact, power-efficient beamforming in next-generation wireless and radar systems.
