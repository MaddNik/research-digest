description: "A new air-clad silicon nitride fabrication process removes electron-beam stitching errors and uses diamond scribing to keep the guided-mode evanescent field accessible for visible-band photonic circuits."
---

Visible-band silicon nitride photonic integrated circuits are conventionally built with a buried oxide overcladding and singulated with wafer-scale tooling, which prevents evanescent access to the guided mode needed for externally integrated emitters. The authors report a PECVD-grown Si3N4 platform built around an air-clad waveguide whose evanescent field stays accessible along the full device length, enabled by two process changes. Fixed-beam moving-stage electron-beam lithography writes 500 nanometer single-mode waveguides as one continuous exposure across a 5 millimeter chip, removing write-field stitching that would otherwise dominate the loss budget at 635 nanometers given the sigma squared over d to the fourth scaling of sidewall scattering in this high-confinement geometry. Chip singulation instead uses pen-type diamond scribing along lithographically patterned markers, cleaving the silicon substrate to produce end-facets within 2 degrees of normal at 80 percent yield. Scanning electron microscopy confirms stitch-free waveguides and undamaged, near-vertical scribed facets, with light coupled end-fire into fabricated devices and guided to a microring, extending monolithic Si3N4 photonics toward scalable visible-to-near-infrared quantum and classical circuits.
