Measurement-device-independent quantum key distribution, or MDI-QKD, removes the need to trust the measurement device, but its performance depends heavily on the quality of the photon source used. This paper compares MDI-QKD built on weak coherent pulses, which are limited by Poissonian photon-number statistics, against quantum dot single-photon sources, which have sub-Poissonian statistics and low multi-photon probability. Using a decoy-state analysis for both source types, the authors find that quantum dot sources outperform even an optimally tuned weak coherent pulse configuration across the full range of transmission distances tested. The analysis also incorporates imperfect photon indistinguishability through Hong-Ou-Mandel interference visibility, showing that both source types are highly sensitive to small deviations from ideal indistinguishability, with significant effects on achievable key rate and maximum distance.
