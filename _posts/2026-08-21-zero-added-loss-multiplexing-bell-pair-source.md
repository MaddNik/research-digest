The paper addresses the goal of increasing entanglement-distribution rates for long-distance quantum networks by multiplexing across frequency modes without incurring the loss penalty typically added by narrowband filtering. The authors demonstrate entanglement swapping across 16 parallel frequency modes using standard 50 gigahertz dense wavelength-division-multiplexing filters, reporting a high average fidelity of 93.9 plus or minus 1.4 percent. Operating the pump pulse at a 3.0 gigahertz repetition frequency, they achieve entanglement swapping rates of 5.38 plus or minus 0.17 pairs per second, which they state corresponds to 820 Bell pairs per second through their zero-added-loss multiplexing technique. The authors conclude that the demonstrated capabilities are key building blocks toward practical quantum repeaters and long-distance quantum networks.
