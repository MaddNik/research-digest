Step-index few-mode fibers provide a compact, passive platform for high-speed photonic information processing by using modal dispersion to map temporal inputs into spatiotemporal representations with short-term memory, but prior demonstrations were limited to linear classification tasks. This paper extends the architecture to nonlinear tasks by adding an optical nonlinearity, implemented with a nonlinear semiconductor optical amplifier (SOA), after the dispersive fiber stage. The authors experimentally evaluate the combined direct and SOA-transformed architecture at 28.5 gigabits per second on delayed XOR and higher-order parity tasks, showing that combining both branches reduces classification errors and broadens the operating range for low-error classification. They further show that exploiting multiple temporal samples per bit period as independent classifier features increases the dimensionality of the photonic representation and improves computational performance.
