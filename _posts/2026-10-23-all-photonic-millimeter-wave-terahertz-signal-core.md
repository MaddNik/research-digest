Electronic signal chains above 100 gigahertz are limited by weak sources, noisy receivers, and lossy interconnects, and prior photonic approaches to this band typically still rely on lossy off-chip electrical or free-space links. The authors present a monolithic lithium tantalate photonic chip, termed a signal core, that integrates generation, modulation, routing, and detection of continuous-wave signals at discrete frequencies between 100 gigahertz and 2.5 terahertz, using exclusively fiber-based optical interfaces. Bidirectional coherent electro-optic conversion supports signal generation, detection, and nanosecond-scale amplitude and phase modulation, while on-chip micro-coplanar striplines interconnect the functional modules into an end-to-end signal chain on a single chip. The modules achieve single-photon electro-optic coupling rates of 10 kilohertz and peak drive-power-normalized photon-flux conversion efficiencies of 1.0 percent per milliwatt for generation and 0.5 percent per milliwatt for detection, and the chip also operates at 4 kelvin in a dilution refrigerator through the same fiber-only links. The authors position this as a broadly applicable, all-photonic platform for millimeter-wave-to-terahertz systems constrained by electrical-interconnect loss, heat load, and footprint.
