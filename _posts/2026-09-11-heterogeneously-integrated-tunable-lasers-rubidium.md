---

The authors introduce a wafer-scalable micro-transfer printing method that integrates GaAs-based optical amplifiers into etched recesses, directly butt-coupled to silicon nitride waveguides, to build compact lasers near the 780 to 800 nanometer band used for rubidium atomic clocks and quantum computing. Their microgear laser reaches a 3 kilohertz fundamental linewidth at more than 22 milliwatts of on-chip output power, a record for a single-mode heterogeneously integrated laser in this band, with a wall-plug efficiency of 9.4 percent. A separate widely tunable laser design using Vernier filters achieves 9 nanometers of coarse tuning, a quasi-continuous fine-tuning range beyond 140 gigahertz, and a mode-hop-free range of 45 gigahertz. The toolkit is intended to replace bulky external-cavity diode lasers in future quantum processors and optical atomic clocks.
