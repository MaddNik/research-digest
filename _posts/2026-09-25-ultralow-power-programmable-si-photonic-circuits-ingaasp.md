Programmable photonic circuits are constrained by the trade-offs of their core building block, the optical phase shifter, among power consumption, speed, modulation efficiency, optical loss, and thermal crosstalk. This work presents a programmable photonic circuit based on InGaAsP and silicon hybrid metal-oxide-semiconductor phase shifters that combine ultralow power consumption, high-speed operation, high modulation efficiency, low optical loss, and negligible thermal crosstalk. The shifters achieve a static power consumption below 30 femtowatts per pi, a switching time of 555 picoseconds, a phase-modulation efficiency of 0.13 V times cm, and a carrier-induced excess insertion loss of only 0.20 decibels per pi. The authors integrate these phase shifters into a programmable Mach-Zehnder interferometer mesh, demonstrating optical switching and programmable unitary transformations with femtowatt-level static power and negligible thermal crosstalk at the circuit level, establishing a foundation for scalable programmable photonic systems.
