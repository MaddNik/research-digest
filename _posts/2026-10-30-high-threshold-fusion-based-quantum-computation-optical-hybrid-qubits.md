The authors introduce hybrid fusion-based quantum computation, which combines small optical resource states that mix discrete-variable and continuous-variable encodings and connects them through hybrid fusion operations. Protocols are developed for generating these resource states directly, without requiring local hybrid logic gates such as the Hadamard gate, alongside a decoding strategy that exploits the error information contained in individual hybrid-fusion outcomes. The scheme achieves a photon loss threshold of 2.231 percent while requiring only 12 hybrid fusions per surface-code unit cell, a substantial improvement over a previous hybrid measurement-based scheme's 0.89 percent threshold and 60-fusion requirement. These results position optical hybrid fusion-based quantum computation as a higher-threshold, more resource-efficient route toward scalable, fault-tolerant photonic quantum computing.
