description: "A new continuous-variable QKD protocol built on displacement receivers and a binary alphabet of noisy coherent states can outperform homodyne detection while remaining secure against collective attacks."
---

The authors turn a binary coherent-state discrimination strategy based on a tunable displacement operation followed by photon detection into a complete continuous-variable quantum key distribution (CV-QKD) protocol using noisy coherent states, that is, displaced thermal states. Because standard quadrature-based channel estimation does not apply to this receiver, they develop a parameter-estimation strategy using the same displacement receiver and show that reliable estimation requires photon-number-resolving detection with sufficient resolution, since finite detector resolution underestimates observed noise and creates a security loophole that they quantify and mitigate. They analyze security against entangling-cloner collective attacks in the asymptotic regime for both direct and reverse reconciliation, showing secret keys can be generated even with noisy state preparation and imperfect detection. The displacement setting that minimizes discrimination error also performs well for key generation, and across a range of transmittances and noise levels the displacement receiver can outperform conventional homodyne detection in experimentally relevant regimes.
