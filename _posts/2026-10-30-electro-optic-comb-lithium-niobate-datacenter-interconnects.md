Growing datacenter interconnect demand from large AI models is pushing operators toward dense wavelength-division multiplexing, and this paper targets that need with an electro-optic frequency comb built on thin-film lithium niobate. The platform combines a flat spectrum, tunable wavelength spacing, and native compatibility with multiplexers and high-speed modulators, addressing weaknesses of earlier Kerr comb and mode-locked laser sources. Using 46 wavelength channels generated from two lasers and a 50 gigahertz on-chip electro-optic comb, the authors demonstrate 16.56 terabits per second of coherent transmission over a 98.84 kilometer deployed fiber link connecting two universities and two datacenters in Hong Kong. They further show the comb can be reconfigured to a 100 gigahertz grid for 10-channel, 80-gigabaud coherent transmission at 6.4 terabits per second, and report a proof-of-concept monolithic transmitter integrating the comb with a high-speed modulator for 19-channel intensity-modulation direct-detection transmission at 1.71 terabits per second. The results support thin-film lithium niobate electro-optic combs as flexible, compact wavelength-division multiplexing engines for both coherent and direct-detection datacenter links.
