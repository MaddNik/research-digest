Squeezed light underlies quantum-enhanced sensing and continuous-variable quantum information, but generating it needs very low optical loss while detecting it needs efficient photon absorption, a materials conflict that has kept these functions on separate chips. This work demonstrates a heterogeneously integrated platform that unifies squeezed-light generation, on-chip routing, and balanced homodyne detection on a single device. The authors measured a two-mode squeezed quantum microcomb spanning 34 quantum modes with approximately 3 decibels of squeezing. Bringing generation, processing, and detection together on one chip removes the need for separate off-chip measurement setups. The authors present the architecture as scalable for future continuous-variable quantum photonic systems.
