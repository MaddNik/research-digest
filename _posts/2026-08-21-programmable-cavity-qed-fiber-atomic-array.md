The paper addresses the challenge of integrating optical tweezer arrays, which give scalable and site-resolved control of neutral atoms, with high-cooperativity cavity quantum electrodynamics systems. The authors combine a twelve-site rubidium-87 optical tweezer array with a high-cooperativity fiber Fabry-Perot microcavity, positioning the array within the cavity mode so that individual sites can be controlled with subwavelength precision. Single-atom coupling strength is continuously tunable through deterministic displacement of atoms within the standing-wave field of the cavity. For up to five atoms coupled to the cavity, the authors measure collectively enhanced vacuum Rabi splitting and implement cavity-based non-destructive readout of the number of coupled atoms. The authors describe this as a scalable architecture for cavity-mediated entanglement generation and many-body cavity quantum electrodynamics with single-atom control, intended as a foundation for fiber-integrated quantum network nodes.
