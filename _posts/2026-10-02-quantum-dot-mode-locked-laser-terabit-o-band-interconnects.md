Practical O-band quantum-dot mode-locked comb sources need wide flat-top bandwidth, low radio-frequency timing noise, error-correctable bare-line modulation, and feedback tolerance together, but these properties are usually optimized one at a time and their link to comb-formation dynamics has been unclear. The authors map the gain-current and saturable-absorber-bias plane of a simple two-section InAs/GaAs quantum-dot mode-locked laser, finding a low-injection high-reverse-bias regime that produces 0.80 picosecond pulses and a high-injection intermediate-bias regime that yields a 16.16 nanometer, 2.75 terahertz flat-top comb with 110 lines, which the authors describe as the broadest reported 3 decibel bandwidth among quantum-dot mode-locked comb sources. At a separate low-noise bias point the beatnote shows a 0.58 kilohertz linewidth and 41.6 femtosecond integrated timing jitter, which they report as the lowest yet seen for a high-channel-count O-band quantum-dot passive comb. In isolator-free 25 gigabit per second NRZ transmission, all 94 carriers across a 13.73 nanometer band stay below the 7 percent hard-decision forward-error-correction threshold, and using all 110 carriers at the maximum-bandwidth bias gives a projected aggregate rate of 2.75 terabits per second. The work links comb-formation physics to distinct, selectable operating regions for isolator-free, terabit-scale O-band optical interconnects.
