Distributed quantum sensing uses entangled probe states to sense combinations of delocalized parameters with better precision than separable states allow, and squeezed light is a leading resource for this because it can be generated and entangled deterministically, though prior demonstrations relied on bulky, hard-to-scale bulk-optic setups. The authors demonstrate entanglement-enhanced sensing of linear functions of four phase shifts using an integrated photonic circuit instead. They measure an entanglement-enhanced precision of 0.199(16) dB below the shot noise limit, compared with only 0.041(18) dB for separable states. A four-mode entangled state is generated on-chip, with entanglement verification and phase sensing also carried out on-chip using an array of four integrated homodyne detectors. The results support integrated photonics as a scalable platform for distributed quantum sensing networks.
