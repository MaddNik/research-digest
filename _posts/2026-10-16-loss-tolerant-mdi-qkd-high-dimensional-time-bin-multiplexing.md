The authors propose a loss-tolerant measurement-device-independent quantum key distribution protocol that uses time-bin-multiplexed single photons entangled with local qubits stored in nitrogen-vacancy centers. In each round, the two end parties each send a single multiplexed photon to an untrusted intermediary, which entangles each photon with its own nitrogen-vacancy qubits and then performs entanglement swapping to establish EPR pairs between the end parties' local qubits. With d time bins per photon, the scheme can generate log base 2 of d plus 1 EPR pairs per transmitted photon, substantially raising the probability of EPR-pair creation under limited channel bandwidth. A memory-insensitive architecture using multiple qubit banks and multiplexed photon routing further reduces the effective memory lifetime requirements, improving loss tolerance and secret key rates over longer distances compared with conventional single-photon protocols. Using the Lo-Chau EPR-QKD framework together with a Shor-Preskill style reduction, the authors prove the protocol is secure without trusting the intermediate node, making it suitable for quantum networks built on untrusted repeaters.
