Stimulated Brillouin scattering (SBS) is a nonlinear interaction between light and sound that is useful for microwave photonic signal processing and narrow-linewidth lasers, but achieving both high Brillouin gain and narrow linewidth on a scalable chip platform has been difficult. The authors introduce a single-crystalline InGaP-on-silicon-dioxide waveguide platform with strong nonlinearities that supports SBS near the 1550 nanometer telecom wavelength. They demonstrate backward SBS with a Brillouin gain coefficient of 588 per watt per meter and a record narrow linewidth of 5.2 megahertz at a 9.346 gigahertz frequency shift. The work establishes a wafer-scale, complementary metal-oxide semiconductor compatible fabrication process for high-gain, narrow-linewidth Brillouin photonics.
