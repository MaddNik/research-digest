Color centers in two-dimensional materials are attractive single-photon sources because they combine high photon brightness with scalable fabrication and easy integration, but realizing high-efficiency sources requires strong radiative emission alongside negligible nonradiative decay. Using first-principles calculations, the authors assess radiative and nonradiative decay for four dopant-vacancy complexes in monolayer silicon carbide, with dopants nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus, and sulfur. All four defects show stable neutral charge states and inter-defect optical transitions. The phosphorus-vacancy complex emerges as the leading single-photon-emitter candidate, combining rapid radiative decay with negligible nonradiative decay and a near-unity quantum yield within the modeled decay channels, while multiphonon decay limits the quantum yield of the nitrogen-vacancy complex despite its high Debye-Waller factor. Hyperfine tensors provide signatures that could help identify the spin-active centers experimentally, and the work proposes a joint radiative-nonradiative screening strategy for designing further solid-state single-photon sources.
