This paper proves expansion properties of superspecial isogeny graphs in dimension g greater than or equal to 2, including graphs with arbitrary level structure, providing explicit bounds on the spectral gap along with a fine characterization of the disconnectedness and periodicity obstructions that level structures can create. For the case without level structure, which was the only case studied previously, the new bounds dramatically improve on the prior state of the art. Isogenies in dimension greater than one have become increasingly important in isogeny-based cryptography, and earlier work had already conjectured consequences of their expansion properties without proving them rigorously. The authors turn those conjectural predecessors into rigorous algorithms, and as an application they give the first algorithm for computing endomorphism rings of superspecial principally polarized abelian varieties in arbitrary dimension, with complexity that is quasi-linear in p to the power g minus 1.
