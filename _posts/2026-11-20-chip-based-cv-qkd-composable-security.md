This paper develops an uncertainty-principle-based security framework for discrete-modulated continuous-variable quantum key distribution that supports finite-size composable key-rate evaluation against collective attacks for modulation constellations up to 256-QAM, a regime where prior composable security analyses became computationally expensive. By adding a heterodyne-measured ancillary mode to the entanglement-based source description, the authors fold extra source correlations into the analysis so the Heisenberg uncertainty principle alone bounds the unknown correlations in the system covariance matrix. Matrix symmetrization reduces the key-rate optimization to a one-dimensional search, and a further mode-reduction step yields an analytical lower bound that needs no numerical search at all. Experiments with 256-QAM on both a discrete-component platform and a silicon-photonic receiver achieve finite-size composable secret key rates of 17.436 and 0.519 megabits per second over a 50 kilometer fiber link. The authors suggest the approach also offers a general way to tighten uncertainty-constrained bounds for other non-Gaussian quantum information tasks.
