The authors numerically demonstrate an asymmetric corner-reflector uni-traveling-carrier waveguide photodiode that simultaneously suppresses optical back-reflection and breaks the conventional bandwidth-responsivity trade-off. Off-center beam coupling introduces geometric asymmetry that eliminates retroreflection while still maintaining efficient light trapping. Three-dimensional optoelectronic simulations show that at 1550 nanometers, a 15 square micrometer device exhibits a 275-gigahertz 3-decibel bandwidth, 0.68 amperes per watt responsivity corresponding to 54.5 percent quantum efficiency, and minus 29-decibel back-reflection, for a 150-gigahertz bandwidth-efficiency product. The design offers a scalable, low-reflection detection scheme intended for sub-terahertz transceivers and dense photonic integration.
