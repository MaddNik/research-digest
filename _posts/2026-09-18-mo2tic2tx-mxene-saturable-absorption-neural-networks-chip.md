MXenes are of interest for integrated nonlinear photonics because of their broadband optical response and chemical tunability, but research has mostly focused on titanium-based compositions, leaving double-transition-metal MXenes largely unexplored. The authors quantify the nonlinear optical response of the double-transition-metal MXene Mo2TiC2Tx and demonstrate its use as a waveguide-integrated nonlinear activation element for neuromorphic photonics. Z-scan measurements at 800 nanometers reveal strong saturable absorption in films of two thicknesses, with effective nonlinear absorption coefficients between about -2.69 x 10^3 and -0.88 x 10^3 centimeters per gigawatt, roughly an order of magnitude larger than reported for Ti3C2Tx MXene under comparable conditions. They integrate an ultrathin Mo2TiC2Tx layer onto a silicon rib waveguide to realize a compact nonlinear optical activation function, which achieves 98.39 percent classification accuracy on the MNIST benchmark when implemented in a neural-network emulator. The work extends the MXene material platform beyond titanium-based compositions for integrated nonlinear photonic and neuromorphic computing.
