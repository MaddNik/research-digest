The authors present a broadband silicon photonics switch that combines micro-electro-mechanical (MEMS) reconfigurability with standard silicon photonic integrated circuits, aimed at large-scale optical circuit switching for data centers and AI or ML clusters. The device is fabricated using a zero-change foundry-compatible process with back-end-of-line post-processing, meaning it avoids custom foundry modifications. It achieves more than 30 decibels of extinction ratio in the C-band, insertion loss below 1.5 decibels, and a static power consumption of about 20 nanowatts at maximum actuation voltage. The authors report that MEMS-based silicon photonics modulators and phase shifters of this kind can operate alongside conventional silicon photonics components in situations demanding small footprint, high extinction ratio, wide bandwidth, and low loss.
