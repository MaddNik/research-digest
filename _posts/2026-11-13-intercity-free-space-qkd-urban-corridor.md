Terrestrial free-space quantum key distribution (QKD) is a flexible complement to fiber networks, but deploying it over long horizontal paths in dense cities is difficult because of atmospheric turbulence, demanding pointing and tracking requirements, and operational constraints. The authors experimentally demonstrate a decoy-state BB84 system operating over an 18.9 kilometer free-space link across a densely populated part of metropolitan Barcelona, using an aviation-compliant optical architecture. The system works at 785 nanometers with room-temperature single-photon detectors and needs no adaptive optics at the receiver, and the modular transmitter is interfaced with a versatile free-space optical terminal and an independent GPS-disciplined synchronization setup. A 40-minute field test showed an average channel attenuation of about 19 decibels and a mean quantum bit error rate of 1.31 percent. From the measured channel parameters, the team estimates an average finite-size secret key rate exceeding 11 kilobits per second, demonstrating that inter-city free-space QKD over dense urban areas is feasible with a comparatively simple architecture.
