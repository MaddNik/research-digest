Lithium niobate on insulator combines strong optical confinement with scalable, high-density fabrication, making it a leading integrated photonics platform, but efficient on-chip laser sources have lagged behind its passive and electro-optic components because lithium niobate's indirect bandgap forces lasing to rely on photoluminescence from rare-earth doping, which has historically had low conversion efficiency. The authors trace this inefficiency to the limited number of rare-earth ions that participate in stimulated emission when comparing LNOI microcavity lasers with fiber lasers and waveguide amplifiers. They demonstrate an integrated erbium-doped LNOI laser that combines highly erbium-doped lithium niobate of high quality, a large-diameter wide microring resonator, a low-loss waveguide amplifier, and bidirectional pumping. This architecture reaches a slope efficiency of 16.91 percent at 1562 nanometers, the first time efficiency above 10 percent has been achieved on the LNOI platform. The result offers a path toward high-efficiency lasers for fully integrated lithium niobate photonic systems.
