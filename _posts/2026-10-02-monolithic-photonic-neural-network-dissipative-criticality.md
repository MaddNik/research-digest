Conventional photonic neural architectures typically fix their computational operations and functional modules in advance, which limits how much a physical system can exploit its own intrinsic dynamics for computation. The authors demonstrate a monolithically integrated active photonic neural network that instead harnesses driven-dissipative criticality, a regime where balancing optical excitation and dissipation amplifies weak input-dependent variations into separable representations while preserving stable attractor dynamics. The chip integrates an optoelectronic nonlinear array with a reconfigurable symmetric optical coupling matrix on silicon photonics, enabling recurrent physical computation in a compact closed loop. It reaches 95.2 percent classification accuracy using just 100 training samples and 44 trainable parameters, and stays robust down to an ultralow signal-to-noise ratio of negative 30 decibels. The same dynamics also support long-range temporal feature extraction from electrocardiogram signals and associative recovery of corrupted patterns through attractor-based relaxation, positioning driven-dissipative criticality as a unified mechanism for representation enhancement, temporal memory, and error-resilient photonic computation.
