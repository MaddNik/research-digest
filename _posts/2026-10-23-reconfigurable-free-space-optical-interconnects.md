Free-space optical interconnects can support many low-data-rate channels while avoiding the cost, energy consumption, and packaging constraints of waveguide- or fiber-based systems. The authors propose a free-space optical interconnect based on reconfigurable micromirror arrays and show that it scales favorably due to two-dimensional scaling, projecting a channel count above 10,000 to achieve 100 terabits per second with slow, sub-10-gigabit-per-second channels. In a proof-of-concept system using a piston-motion micromirror array, they demonstrate nine simultaneously optimized channels, achieving -21.7 decibels of average worst-case crosstalk for a channel pitch equal to twice the beam radius, which is a smaller spacing than required by flat tip-tilt mirrors. The optimization is further investigated across different channel spacings, simultaneous illumination conditions, and output counts, generally achieving smaller worst-case crosstalk than the flat tip-tilt mirror case. Overall, the work demonstrates an advantage of piston-motion micromirrors over flat tip-tilt mirrors for scalable free-space optical interconnects.
