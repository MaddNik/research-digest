Multipair emission from spontaneous parametric down-conversion sources is a principal source of false heralds in entanglement swapping, because a conventional Bell-state measurement cannot distinguish a genuine photon pair from two neighboring sources from a mixed-polarization double emission by one source plus vacuum from the other. The authors show that a cascaded network can resolve this ambiguity by letting successive swapping stations perform different, complementary measurements: a direct-basis Bell-state measurement rejects same-polarization double emissions, while a balanced equatorial analyzer built from a four-mode Green Machine rejects mixed-polarization double emissions using two-photon interference. Using a Gaussian-state analysis exact to all orders of multipair emission, they evaluate three-source and four-source networks under coupling, detector, and channel loss, finding that the four-source alternating chain heralds an exact Bell state in the lossless limit. The fidelity advantage over source-count-matched all-Bell-state-measurement networks persists at every studied loss level, and the approach yields a single-channel rate advantage without requiring multiplexing.
