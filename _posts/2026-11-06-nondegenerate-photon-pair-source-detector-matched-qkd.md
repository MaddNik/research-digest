In entanglement-based quantum key distribution, the single-photon detectors are usually the largest hardware cost, since both photons of each pair typically need a telecom-wavelength detector. This paper studies a highly nondegenerate photon-pair source using two periodically poled KTiOPO4 crystals pumped at 405 nanometers that emit pairs at 548 and 1550 nanometers, so only the 1550-nanometer photon needs to travel through fiber and only the 548-nanometer arm needs a telecom-grade detector. A silicon single-photon avalanche diode with 82 percent efficiency detects the 548-nanometer photon at the source node. An unoptimized module generates about 1.5 million pairs per second per milliwatt of pump power, collecting 13 percent of pairs on the 548-nanometer arm and 23 percent on the 1550-nanometer arm. Comparing against generic two-telecom-photon links in an asymptotic BBM92 model, the authors estimate that with optimistic collection and brightness targets, their cryostat-free link could cost about 70 percent less per secret bit than a generic link using one superconducting nanowire detector.
