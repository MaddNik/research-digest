Conventional network threat detection relies on packet-level, flow-level, or host-level telemetry, but the authors investigate a different observation surface: unintended electromagnetic emissions generated by network interface card activity. They present NICWhisper, which externally captures NIC electromagnetic emissions, converts them into time-frequency representations, and recognizes network behaviors without inspecting packet contents or host-side runtime state, exploiting how traffic-driven timing, rate, concurrency, and burst organization shape the measured emissions. The authors construct a NIC electromagnetic dataset covering benign workloads and seven representative threat behaviors under diverse execution conditions, and NICWhisper achieves 80.67 percent Macro-F1 across the eight behavior classes. Further experiments show the extracted behavior-related information extends beyond simple signal magnitude and remains partially transferable across execution conditions and different NIC hardware, positioning NIC electromagnetic leakage as a complementary physical observation source for network security monitoring when direct access to traffic or host telemetry is limited.
