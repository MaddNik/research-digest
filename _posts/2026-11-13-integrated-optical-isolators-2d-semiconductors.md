Integrated optical isolators based on spin-momentum locking could protect photonic integrated circuits from unwanted reflections, but today's most common implementations are magneto-optical isolators that require an externally applied magnetic field. The authors note that a wide range of nonreciprocal effects in alternative material platforms remains unexploited, and that effects which change the propagation of light as a function of polarization can be connected to propagation direction through spin-momentum locking. They use a computational approach to study various designs of integrated isolators based on nonreciprocal 2D semiconductors, examining how geometry and optical properties affect device performance. They also propose a new concept for an all-optical isolator based on spin-valley polarization and optically tunable magnetism in transition metal dichalcogenide monolayers. The results establish 2D semiconductors as a platform for integrated nonreciprocal devices that can enable functionalities, such as all-optical operation, that are difficult to achieve with conventional magneto-optical materials.
