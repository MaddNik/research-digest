---

Hybrid III-V/silicon quantum dot microring lasers are compact and energy efficient, but their whispering-gallery cavities are naturally multimode and bidirectional, producing mode hopping that is incompatible with dense wavelength-division multiplexing. The authors show that an azimuthal grating patterned into the silicon ring, a single lithographic design choice, converts the cavity into a wavelength-addressed single-mode source by replacing the degenerate counter-propagating mode pair with symmetric and anti-symmetric standing-wave supermodes of unequal radiative loss. Devices fabricated on a 100 millimeter silicon-on-insulator platform achieve a side-mode suppression ratio of 37.9 dB with continuous, hop-free tuning, and the lasing wavelength is fixed lithographically rather than by the gain peak, reaching a minimum threshold current of 1.95 milliamps near 50 degrees Celsius. Combined with multi-gigahertz direct modulation, the authors present these lasers as practical building blocks for cascaded, wavelength-addressed transmitter arrays in data communication and co-packaged optics.
