Multiphoton graph states are an important resource for measurement-based quantum computing and memory-less quantum repeaters, but scalable generation of large graphs with programmable connections has remained an outstanding challenge, since conventional probabilistic fusion protocols require heavy hardware overhead to become deterministic. The authors introduce a deterministic fusion operation with built-in error mitigation that connects small time-bin-encoded cluster states, originally generated on demand from a quantum emitter, into larger reconfigurable graph states. The fusion is realized by nondestructively projecting pairs of flying microwave photonic qubits onto Bell states using superconducting circuits with in-situ programmability. Crucially, the protocol actively resets and reuses the matter qubits during graph-state generation, circumventing the coherence limits of the quantum emitter and enabling genuine multipartite entanglement across 13 photonic qubits. The result, published in Nature Physics, establishes a scalable framework for photonic graph-state synthesis aimed at fault-tolerant measurement-based quantum computation and distributed quantum networks.
