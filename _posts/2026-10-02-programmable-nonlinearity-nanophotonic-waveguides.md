description: "Researchers engineer electrically programmable quasi-phase matching inside nanophotonic waveguides, letting a chip's nonlinear optical function be reconfigured after fabrication."
---

Nonlinear nanophotonic devices usually need quasi-phase matching baked in at fabrication time, for example by poling the nonlinear material with fixed electrode geometry, which locks in the optical function and leaves performance exposed to fabrication imperfections. This work instead builds programmable chi-squared holograms inside nanophotonic waveguides by projecting structured optical illumination onto photoconductive electrodes, creating spatial patterns of electric-field-induced nonlinearity that can be updated in about one second after the device is made. The induced patterns give spectral, modal, and polarization control over three-wave mixing processes in the waveguide. The authors use closed-loop in situ optimization to compensate for phase mismatch in complex structures such as width-modulated and multi-centimeter spiral waveguides, and show the same programmability enables on-chip diagnostics like direct dispersion measurement and wavefront phase tomography. The approach points toward cascaded nonlinear photonic systems that sidestep fabrication-driven yield issues while keeping the benefits of etched waveguides, such as confinement and dispersion engineering.
