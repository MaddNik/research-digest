Diffractive optical processors for optical computing typically rely on spatially distributed trainable degrees of freedom, leading to extended architectures. The authors instead propose a compact neuromorphic optical-computing approach where the entire trainable transformation is implemented by a single Mie scatterer, formulating computation in a vector spherical harmonics basis so that trainable modal couplings are concentrated in the object's T-matrix, whose parameter count scales as the fourth power of the maximal multipole order. They demonstrate classification of phase-encoded MNIST images using scattered-field intensity, reaching about 90 percent test accuracy at a particle size parameter of ka equals 15, comparable to a single-layer artificial neural network, with similar performance achievable using near-fields for on-chip integration. The authors also analyze how reciprocity, passivity, and particle symmetry constrain performance, and inverse-design a non-absorbing dielectric scatterer that performs the classification task with 84 percent accuracy.
