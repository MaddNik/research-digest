Researchers present the first chip-scale space-time multiplexed Gaussian boson sampling (GBS) system, integrating high-speed electro-optic modulators, on-chip delay lines, and a time-space multiplexed interferometric network on a single thin-film lithium niobate chip. The system operates at a 4 gigahertz clock rate and records detection events with up to 11,059 photons within one millisecond, addressing the optical alignment and phase instability problems that limit conventional bulk GBS setups. Beyond benchmarking quantum computational advantage, the authors reconfigure the same photonic hardware into a GBS-powered world model for predicting physical dynamics, achieving lower prediction error with fewer trainable readout parameters than a classical echo state network baseline. The results demonstrate a scalable, wafer-compatible route toward programmable photonic quantum hardware.
