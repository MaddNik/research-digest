Free carriers generated by two-photon absorption normally limit optical parametric oscillation in silicon at telecom wavelengths. The authors demonstrate telecom-band parametric oscillation in a passive silicon microring resonator driven by a single continuous-wave pump at 4.5 kelvin, without any electrical carrier sweep-out mechanism. The device reaches an on-chip oscillation onset power of 2.25 milliwatts and a total conversion efficiency of 1.9 percent into all parametric sidebands at a pump power of 4.8 milliwatts. Despite continuous-wave pumping, the parametric output appears as a train of pulses synchronized with microsecond-scale self-pulsing, and at higher pump powers the oscillation is absent during intervals of sustained sub-gigahertz oscillations in the pump transmission. Tuning the pump detuning extends the oscillation to temperatures up to 55 kelvin. The results show that nonlinear cavity dynamics govern the operating regime of cryogenic silicon optical parametric oscillators that are compatible with cryogenic quantum photonic circuits.
