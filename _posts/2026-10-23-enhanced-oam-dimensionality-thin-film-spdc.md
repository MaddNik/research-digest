Thin-film spontaneous parametric down-conversion (SPDC) relaxes the phase-matching condition and generates photon pairs that are extremely broadband in frequency and angle and highly entangled in time, frequency, position, and momentum. The authors show that thin-film SPDC also produces a very broad orbital-angular-momentum (OAM) spectrum that, unlike in bulk crystals, is decoupled from the frequency spectrum. They demonstrate this experimentally by generating photon pairs in a 7-micrometer-thick lithium niobate film and comparing them against a 500-micrometer-thick periodically poled lithium niobate crystal under identical pump conditions. The thin-film source exhibits a substantially broader OAM spectrum, with an azimuthal Schmidt number almost nine times larger than the bulk source, and quantum-state tomography on selected two- and four-dimensional OAM subspaces demonstrates high-purity maximally entangled states. The results position thin nonlinear films as a promising platform for generating high-dimensional quantum states with weak coupling between spatial and spectral degrees of freedom.
