---

Inverse design produces unintuitive but powerful nanophotonic devices, but it has been limited by costly fabrication iterations, simulation-reality gaps, and the fixed function of etched devices. The authors demonstrate in situ training of a single free-form programmable-photonic device, a thin-film lithium niobate slab waveguide with full programmability over the refractive index across roughly ten thousand effective pixels, to realize a variety of functions that would conventionally require separate devices. They show 4 by 4 and 8 by 8 Gaussian-mode permutations and 4 by 4 Hermite-Gaussian mode demultiplexing with crosstalk as low as negative 27 decibels, plus 4 by 4 matrix-vector multiplication, wavelength-independent operation across the 1500 to 1600 nanometer window, and wavelength demultiplexing between 1310 and 1550 nanometers. Because the device is reconfigured after fabrication for each function, the cost of fabrication, characterization, and calibration can be amortized across all the photonic functions the device ultimately performs.
