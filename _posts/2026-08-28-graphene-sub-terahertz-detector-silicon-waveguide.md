The authors address the open challenge of integrating room-temperature terahertz photodetectors directly onto low-loss dielectric waveguides, a step that is routine in near- and mid-infrared photonic integrated circuits but has previously required hybrid flip-chip assembly in the terahertz range. They demonstrate a graphene sub-terahertz detector monolithically integrated on a high-resistivity silicon dielectric waveguide for D-band operation between 110 and 170 gigahertz, coupling an hBN-encapsulated graphene channel to the guided mode through a tapered slot-line antenna patterned directly on the silicon surface. The detector achieves a voltage responsivity of 11.5 volts per watt and a 3 dB bandwidth of 1.94 gigahertz, with the bandwidth currently limited by parasitic inductance of the readout interconnects rather than the intrinsic graphene response. The authors present this as a practical pathway toward fully integrated room-temperature terahertz photonic circuits, with electrostatic gating and impedance matching expected to improve responsivity and bandwidth by roughly an order of magnitude.
