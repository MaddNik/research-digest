BBM92, introduced by Bennett, Brassard, and Mermin in 1992, is one of the most widely adopted quantum key distribution protocols, and like any such protocol its practical security depends on satisfying the assumptions behind its theoretical security proofs. The authors examine two common BBM92 receiver implementations, a conventional passive receiver and a time-multiplexed configuration that halves the number of detectors, characterizing the passive polarization analyzers common to both. They present experimental results from the time-multiplexed receiver using a continuous-wave pumped spontaneous parametric down-conversion source of polarization-entangled photon pairs and threshold detectors. The paper discusses the specific challenges encountered during post-processing when trying to meet the requirements of existing security proofs, and more broadly the gap between theoretical security assumptions and the extraction of a provably secure cryptographic key in a real system.
