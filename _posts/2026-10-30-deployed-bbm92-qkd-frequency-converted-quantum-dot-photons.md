This paper reports a field deployment of entanglement-based quantum key distribution using a droplet-etched quantum dot operating near 780 nanometers as the entangled-photon source. Quantum frequency conversion interfaces the emitted photons with the telecommunication wavelength range while preserving their polarization entanglement, allowing use of standard deployed fiber. Running the BBM92 protocol over a 1.59 kilometer field-deployed link across the University of Stuttgart's Vaihingen campus, the authors achieve a secure key rate of 206 hertz over 19 hours of stable operation, producing a total secure key length of 14.2 megabits. The resulting key is used to encrypt and decrypt a color image after error correction and privacy amplification, and the work combines a high-excitation-rate entangled-photon source, an efficient frequency conversion module, and picosecond-level time synchronization to demonstrate a step toward hybrid quantum networks.
