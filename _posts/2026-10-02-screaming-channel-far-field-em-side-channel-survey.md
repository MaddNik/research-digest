The widespread deployment of IoT devices makes them attractive targets, and radio-coupling-based far-field EM side-channel attacks, also called screaming channel attacks, have emerged over the past decade as a threat that exploits unintended coupling between digital computation and a device's RF transmission chain, letting sensitive information be captured from radiated radio signals at long distances rather than through conventional power or near-field EM leakage. The authors note that despite real practical demonstrations of remote screaming-channel attacks, the literature on the topic remains fragmented, so they present the first systematic survey of screaming channel attack methodologies and case studies, organizing prior work into a hierarchical framework covering leakage discovery and understanding, enhanced attack capability, attack targets and variants, and public data collections. Through a fine-grained cross-paper analysis, they compare representative works by target implementation, attack distance, analysis method, profiling requirement, and acquisition environment, among other factors. The survey distills this body of work into one central trade-off, three major limitations, and three future research roadmaps, aiming to give both a structured understanding of this rapidly developing attack class and practical guidance for evaluating its real-world threat boundaries.
