The authors give a public-key encryption scheme that is provably secure against polynomial-size adversaries, assuming super-polylogarithmic hardness of the standard planted clique conjecture for any exponent strictly between 0 and 1, together with a relatively mild hardness conjecture about noisy k-LIN over expanders that is not known by itself to imply public-key encryption. Both underlying conjectures correspond to natural average-case variants of NP-complete problems that have been studied for multiple decades, with unconditional lower bounds supporting them in a variety of restricted models of computation. The resulting encryption scheme answers an open question posed in a seminal work by Applebaum, Barak, and Wigderson at STOC 2010, showing that the planted clique conjecture, long studied mainly for its relevance to average-case complexity and statistical algorithms, can serve as a foundation for public-key cryptography when combined with this expander-based assumption.
