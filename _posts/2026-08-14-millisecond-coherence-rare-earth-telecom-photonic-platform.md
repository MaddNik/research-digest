---

The authors address quantum-network hardware requirements by integrating an erbium-doped (Er3+:CaWO4) crystal directly onto a lithium niobate microring resonator without an intervening adhesive layer. Operating at 75 millikelvin and a 0.2 tesla magnetic field, the hybrid device achieved an effective homogeneous linewidth of 289 plus or minus 34 hertz, corresponding to millisecond-scale coherence times. The lithium niobate resonator's electro-optic tunability revealed strong collective light-matter coupling, with a cooperativity of 6.7 plus or minus 0.4. Exploiting nuclear spin coupling, the team also demonstrated optical phase storage and retrieval over 5 seconds with high fidelity. The authors argue this heterogeneous integration of a coherence-optimized rare-earth crystal with a photonic chip is a viable path toward scalable telecom-wavelength quantum memories and networks.
