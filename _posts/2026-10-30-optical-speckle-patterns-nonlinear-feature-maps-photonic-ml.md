This work investigates an optical computing architecture built around an integrated photonic circular multipass cell, aimed at energy-efficient machine learning and pattern recognition. The system's information-processing capacity is characterized through measures of nonlinearity and cosine similarity, which capture how distinguishable the generated feature representations are. Numerical results show that reservoir mirror reflectance, the size of the encoded figure, and the refractive-index contrast between the reservoir and the encoded figure all tune the system's nonlinearity, letting the generated features be adjusted through these parameters. Applying the resulting speckle-based representations to classification of texture images and thyroid collagen images yields accuracies ranging from 69 to 93 percent, suggesting that such photonic reservoir systems could underpin more energy-efficient machine-learning hardware.
