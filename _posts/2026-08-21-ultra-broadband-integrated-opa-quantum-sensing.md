The authors address a stated challenge in integrated nonlinear photonics: achieving simultaneous ultra-broadband and high-gain optical parametric amplification on a single chip, despite thin-film lithium niobate's known strengths for signal generation and nonlinear interactions. They demonstrate a continuous-wave-pumped optical parametric amplifier device with a reported wavelength window of about 450 nanometers, corresponding to roughly 56 terahertz of bandwidth. The device can be operated either through direct visible-wavelength pumping or through a cascaded telecom-band pumping scheme, yielding maximum gains of 8.87 decibels and 10.79 decibels respectively. The authors position this integrated amplifier as a building block for next-generation quantum sensing and broader photonic system applications.
