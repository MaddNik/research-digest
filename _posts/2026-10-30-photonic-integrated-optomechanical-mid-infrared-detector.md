The authors remove a key bottleneck in nanomechanical thermal detection, which normally requires free-space or fiber-coupled interferometers to track resonance frequency shifts, by monolithically integrating a high-quality-factor nanomechanical resonator with a silicon nitride photonic integrated circuit. A tensile-stressed silicon nitride trampoline carrying an impedance-matched platinum absorber is dispersively coupled to a photonic integrated microresonator positioned beneath it. At room temperature and microwatt-level optical power, the detector reaches a noise-equivalent power of 3.4 picowatts per square root hertz for broadband thermal radiation between 3 and 12 micrometers, within a factor of 1.5 of its fundamental thermal fluctuation limit when referred to absorbed power. Used inside a commercial Fourier-transform infrared spectrometer, the detector resolves the vibrational fingerprint of a polymer film across 2 to 20 micrometers at about one order of magnitude lower incident power than the instrument's built-in pyroelectric detector, opening a path toward multiplexed optomechanical focal-plane arrays.
