This work presents a multimode continuous-variable quantum key distribution architecture that pairs silicon-photonic transmitter and receiver chips with CMOS electronics, using polarization multiplexing as a two-mode demonstration of the approach. The receiver chip is co-integrated with a dual-channel 28-nanometer CMOS transimpedance amplifier that provides shot-noise clearances exceeding 20 decibels on both channels, and pilot-assisted unitary digital recovery removes the need for active optical polarization control. A multimode security analysis that accounts for mode crosstalk shows that at 2 percent residual crosstalk the secure distance extends from 39 to 84 kilometers, approaching the crosstalk-free limit of 89 kilometers. Over 10 kilometers of deployed fiber, the system achieves a mean total finite-size secret key rate of 6.69 megabits per second, 12 percent above what a single-mode analysis would estimate from the same data. The authors present this as a route toward scalable, high-throughput quantum key distribution networks built on hardware suited to volume manufacturing.
