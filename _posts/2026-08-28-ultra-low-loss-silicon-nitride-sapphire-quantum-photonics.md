This paper investigates sapphire as an alternative substrate for silicon nitride (Si3N4) photonic integrated circuits, aiming to overcome the long-wavelength transparency limits, high-stress deposition, and leakage-loss drawbacks of standard silicon dioxide on silicon substrates. The authors fabricate a 500 nanometer thick Si3N4-on-sapphire platform achieving record-low propagation losses below 0.1 dB/cm. High-Q microrings on this platform reach intrinsic quality factors above 4.5 million, and coupled-ring photonic molecules are used to support nonlinear gain. Building on this low loss and high Q, the work reports the first demonstration of Kerr-comb and soliton generation on the Si3N4-on-sapphire platform. The authors argue these advances open a path toward spectroscopy and quantum-enhanced sensing across spectral regions previously inaccessible to standard Si3N4 photonics.
