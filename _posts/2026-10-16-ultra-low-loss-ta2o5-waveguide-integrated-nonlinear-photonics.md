Integrated nonlinear photonics needs waveguides that simultaneously offer ultra-low loss, relatively strong nonlinearity, and tight mode confinement, and tantalum pentoxide (Ta2O5) is attractive because it combines a broad transparency window, decent third-order nonlinearity, and favorable thick-film growth. The authors mitigate the usual trade-off between tight confinement and sidewall scattering loss by fabricating pure Ta2O5 waveguides using a Damascene-based process. The resulting microring resonators, with a cross-section of 1.6 micrometers by 0.85 micrometers, reach intrinsic quality factors above 4.0 times 10 to the sixth power, corresponding to propagation losses below 10 decibels per meter, while keeping the anomalous dispersion needed for bright dissipative Kerr soliton operation. Using these resonators the team generates a coherent single-soliton microcomb with a 108.32 gigahertz repetition rate and, under dual-pump operation, observes a single-soliton detuning window spanning 7.5 gigahertz, a large tolerance attributed to the material's weak thermo-optic response. The work establishes pure Ta2O5 as an ultra-low-loss platform foundation for chip-scale nonlinear photonic systems, and the paper is accepted for publication in Optica.
