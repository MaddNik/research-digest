description: "Careful timing of photon detection windows, rather than spectral filtering, lets continuous-wave-driven silicon nitride microresonators produce highly indistinguishable heralded photons for quantum networks."
---

Quantum networks need scalable photon sources that combine narrow linewidth, high efficiency, and high indistinguishability, and microresonator photon-pair sources are attractive candidates even though a source-only joint spectral amplitude description predicts near-zero Hong-Ou-Mandel interference visibility for photons from independent continuous-wave-driven microresonators. The authors show that the observed interference is not determined by the joint spectral amplitude alone, and by combining finite-time detection with cavity-enhanced spontaneous four-wave mixing they characterize a detector-conditioned heralded state governed by the idler-photon detection window. Independently optimizing the idler and signal detection windows lets both heralded-photon indistinguishability and intrinsic heralding efficiency approach unity without spectral filtering or complex source engineering. Using integrated high-Q silicon nitride microresonators, they measure Hong-Ou-Mandel visibilities of 0.992(8) and 0.942(12), without background subtraction, at fourfold count rates of 4.5(3) and 12.2(6) hertz. The work establishes continuous-wave-driven high-Q microresonators as a robust, scalable platform for quantum-network primitives.
