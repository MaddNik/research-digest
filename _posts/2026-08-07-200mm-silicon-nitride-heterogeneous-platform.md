The authors describe a dual-layer low-pressure chemical vapor deposition silicon nitride photonics platform built on a 200 millimeter CMOS pilot line, targeting applications such as optical interconnects, quantum computing, augmented and virtual reality, and medical diagnostics that need low-loss visible and near-infrared photonics. The platform integrates micro-transfer printing modules as a back-end post-processing step, so active components can be added without disrupting the main fabrication flow, along with a hydrogenated amorphous silicon layer that supports evanescently coupled III-V lasers and other passive functions. Wafer-scale measurements show silicon nitride propagation losses of 4 decibels per centimeter at 488 nanometers and 0.23 decibels per centimeter at 940 nanometers, with only 0.35 decibels of transition loss between the silicon nitride and amorphous silicon layers, matching simulation. As a demonstration of active functionality, gallium arsenide-based gain sections were micro-transfer printed onto multiple dies, achieving consistent die-to-die lasing at 970 nanometers with on-chip optical power near 1 milliwatt.
