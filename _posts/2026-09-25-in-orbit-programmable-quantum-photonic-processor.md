---

Quantum states of light have already been generated and transmitted from orbit for secure communication and fundamental tests, but they had never been used as a computational resource in space until now. The authors report a programmable quantum photonic platform operating on a nanosatellite that processes two photons in a six-mode universal integrated circuit. By programming distinct unitaries and tuning the photons into indistinguishability, they observe two-photon interference, demonstrating on-board generation, manipulation, and detection of non-classical light despite the thermal drifts, radiation, and rocket launch a space mission entails. The authors frame this as extending space-based technologies toward in-orbit quantum-assisted computing, for example local encoding of Earth-observation data or acting as a node in a distributed quantum network.
