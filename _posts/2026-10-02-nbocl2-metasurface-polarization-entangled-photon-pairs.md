---

Compact sources of polarization-entangled photon pairs with controllable entanglement remain hard to build, and van der Waals nonlinear materials such as NbOCl2 provide atomically thin platforms for quantum light generation but their native crystalline anisotropy restricts which quantum states are accessible. The authors develop a resonant van der Waals nonlinear metasurface based on NbOCl2 that exploits this intrinsic optical anisotropy to engineer polarization-dependent nonlinear responses, with the anisotropic dispersion enabling selective manipulation of resonant modes and a three-order-of-magnitude enhancement of the nonlinear response along the crystal's c axis. By further tailoring these resonant modes, the effective second-order nonlinear susceptibility tensor is redistributed between orthogonal polarization channels, balancing spontaneous parametric down-conversion along the b and c axes. This produces polarization-entangled photon generation with a measured fidelity of up to 92 percent. The work establishes metasurface-enabled nonlinear optical engineering as a strategy for controlling quantum light generation in van der Waals materials, pointing toward scalable quantum photonic platforms.
