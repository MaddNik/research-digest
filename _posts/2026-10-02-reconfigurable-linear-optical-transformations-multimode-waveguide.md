Wavefront shaping normally relies on free-space setups built from bulk optical components, so compact, fully integrated versions have been comparatively unexplored. The authors introduce a single multimode waveguide with distributed thermo-optic perturbations for controlling multimode propagation, paired with a differentiable physical model that numerically shapes the outgoing wavefront and programs linear optical transformations through gradient-based optimization. The approach achieves diffraction-limited focusing across 20 spatial modes while retaining more than 99 percent of the input optical power, and it extends to complete input-output transformations, implementing Sylvester-Hadamard matrix operations up to 32 by 32 with correlations exceeding 99 percent. Reducing the number of tunable elements requires stronger individual perturbations and causes broader mode coupling beyond the target modes, so the authors use ancillary modes to realize a 4 by 4 transformation reaching 93.8 plus or minus 4.8 percent correlation with only 5 times N squared tunable elements, and their best realization reaches 98.3 percent correlation with the target. The results show thermo-optic control of multimode waveguides can enable fully integrated wavefront shaping and programmable linear optics for both classical and quantum photonic processing.
