---

Efficient frequency conversion inside optical fibers is desirable for nonlinear and quantum photonic technologies but is limited by weak nonlinear interactions and poor coupling into fiber. The authors demonstrate resonantly enhanced second-harmonic generation using an all-fiber-integrated gallium nitride hole-type circular Bragg grating cavity transferred directly onto a standard optical fiber. The cavity exploits gallium nitride's large second-order nonlinear susceptibility and wide transparency window, and its membrane geometry provides strong field confinement plus vertically directional out-coupling of the generated signal. The resulting second-harmonic signal is described as drastically enhanced compared with bulk gallium nitride or an unpatterned freestanding membrane. Using a deterministic pick-and-place transfer technique, the authors achieve robust, precise integration of the cavity device onto conventional fiber, establishing what they describe as a compact and scalable approach to adding optical nonlinearity to fiber-based photonic systems.
