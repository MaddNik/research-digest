On-chip wavelength-division multiplexing for broadband operation is fundamentally limited by the narrow free spectral range and inefficient drop transmission of conventional optical microcavities. This work presents a scalable integrated WDM processing platform based on cascaded dual-sided coupled Fabry-Perot add-drop resonators that achieves a record-large free-spectral-range-free bandwidth of more than 300 nanometers with low insertion loss below 0.5 decibels, while independently manipulating monochromatic light in both wavelength and intensity. Programmable cores with up to 20 channels in a single bus waveguide are configured as a high-capacity WDM communication fabric supporting 2.4 terabit-per-second single-link transmission and O-to-C-band multi-subband (de)triplexing, a reconfigurable optical processor with broadband and hitless wavelength-selective switching, and a parallel computing accelerator theoretically capable of 1.28 tera-operations-per-second convolution with 16-channel modulation. The authors present this as a unified platform bridging high-speed optical communication with in-line computing.
