This work extends the spatial photonic Ising machine (SPIM) platform, which exploits optical parallelism for Ising computation, to long-range Ising models with dense nonlocal and competing interactions. The authors present a momentum-space-engineered SPIM framework that maps prescribed long-range coupling kernels onto momentum-space masks for parallel Hamiltonian evaluation, based on a high-fidelity optical field propagation model. They systematically investigate annealing dynamics for power-law interactions, examining how the critical temperature depends on the decay exponent and coupling cutoff radius, and for RKKY interactions, reproducing diverse ordered states arising from competing long-range couplings. A proof-of-principle experiment confirms the physical feasibility of the approach, broadening the class of many-body systems accessible to photonic Ising machines.
