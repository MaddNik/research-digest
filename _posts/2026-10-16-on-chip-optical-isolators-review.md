This review addresses the long-standing challenge of building a broadly adopted on-chip optical isolator that combines strong isolation, low insertion loss, broad bandwidth, compact footprint, and scalable fabrication. The authors first distinguish pairwise nonreciprocity from intensity-asymmetric transmission and from true isolation using constitutive relations and the scattering matrix, clarifying what each proposed solution actually achieves. They then compare three physical routes to breaking Lorentz reciprocity on chip: magneto-optical bias, externally driven space-time modulation, and nonlinear optical processes. Topological and non-Hermitian systems are treated as cross-cutting design frameworks that can route or enhance a nonreciprocal response but do not themselves break reciprocity. The paper closes by examining fundamental trade-offs among isolation strength, insertion loss, bandwidth, footprint, modal coverage, drive requirements, and fabrication compatibility, giving a mechanism-based perspective for designing isolators for scalable photonic integrated circuits.
