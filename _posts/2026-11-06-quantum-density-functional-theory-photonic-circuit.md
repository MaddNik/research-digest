The authors present and experimentally validate a Quantum Density Functional Theory, or Q-DFT, algorithm for simulating molecular electronic structure that requires a number of qubits growing only as the base-two logarithm of the number of orbitals, rather than scaling linearly as standard approaches do. They solve the Kohn-Sham problem for the lithium hydride molecule using an ensemble extension of the variational quantum eigensolver that minimizes energy across the subspace spanned by the ground and first excited states. The problem is encoded on a quantum photonic platform that combines a deterministic quantum dot single-photon source with a programmable integrated photonic circuit. By evaluating both ground-state and first-excited-state molecular properties, the work demonstrates a qubit-efficient algorithm running on reconfigurable photonic hardware. The authors present this as a pathway toward simulating larger molecular systems with near-term quantum photonic chemistry hardware.
