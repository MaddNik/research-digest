Researchers demonstrate a silicon nitride (Si3N4) ring microresonator that operates as a frequency-degenerate twin-photon source at telecommunication wavelengths, converting two photons from spectrally distinct pump beams into a pair of identical twin photons via an inverse four-wave mixing process. The measurements show a maximum coincidence-to-accidental ratio of 5.4 plus or minus 0.6. The same microresonator also functions as a heralded single-photon source through pump-degenerate spontaneous four-wave mixing, with a measured spectral purity of 0.67 plus or minus 0.05 and a heralded anti-bunching value of 0.0042 plus or minus 0.0015. This is the first demonstration of both photon-generation schemes on a single integrated Si3N4 platform, and the authors say it points toward scalable, tailored quantum light generation on chip.
