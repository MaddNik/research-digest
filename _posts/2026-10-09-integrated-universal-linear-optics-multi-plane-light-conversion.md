---

Integrated universal linear optical processors built from multi-plane light conversion (MPLC) offer low hardware complexity and high fabrication robustness, but have lacked validated calibration protocols to realize their full potential. The authors propose a machine-learning-based calibration method and verify it on an eight-mode silicon photonic processor built from interleaved multiport directional couplers and phase-shifter arrays, characterizing coupler transfer matrices and individual phase-shifter responses. They validate robustness by demonstrating universal operation with Haar-random unitary matrices across three wavelengths and under partial phase-shifter deactivation. With this fully characterized, "white-boxed" device, they further demonstrate high-dimensional quantum gates and quantum reservoir computing applied to handwritten digit classification, advancing the case for large-scale, fabrication-robust broadband photonic hardware for both classical and quantum information processing.
