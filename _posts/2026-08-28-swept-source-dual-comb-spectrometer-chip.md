Dual-comb spectroscopy resolves narrow molecular absorption features by interfering two mutually detuned frequency combs, but closely spaced comb lines normally require large laser cavities with long round-trip times, which has hindered miniaturization. The authors circumvent this by demonstrating a swept-source dual-comb spectrometer using two unidirectional racetrack semiconductor lasers on the same chip, generating traveling-wave frequency combs through strong RF injection at a 14.3 gigahertz cavity round-trip frequency and tuning them continuously via the laser drive currents. This enables continuous spectral sampling over a 32 per-centimeter range centered near 8 micrometers with an effective sampling interval of about 80 megahertz, and both comb outputs share a monolithically integrated light coupler that automatically aligns them before they interrogate the sample. The spectrometer's performance is benchmarked against an external-cavity tunable laser and validated using HITRAN simulations for 1.25 percent nitrous oxide, and the unidirectional comb architecture is shown to suppress the detrimental effects of optical feedback.
