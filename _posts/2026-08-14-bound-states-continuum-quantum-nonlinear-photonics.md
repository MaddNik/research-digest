The authors study bound states in the continuum (BICs) in integrated silicon nitride microresonators as a tool for spontaneous four-wave mixing. They create electrically tunable Friedrich-Wintgen BICs with quality factors exceeding one million in an interferometrically coupled resonator; although the BIC mode cannot be excited directly, it becomes populated via four-wave mixing and is accessed through its radiative partner mode. In the weak-signal, quantum regime they observe distinctive correlation signatures between signal and idler photons, with coherence properties approaching the limits set by intrinsic loss. At higher optical power, using the BIC as one of the parametric amplification modes increased gain by 12 decibels and lowered the oscillation threshold by 1.6 decibels. The work establishes BICs as a general mechanism for engineering mode lifetimes to control both biphoton generation and classical parametric thresholds on the same integrated platform.
