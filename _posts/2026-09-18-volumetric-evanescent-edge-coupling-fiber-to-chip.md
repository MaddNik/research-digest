description: "A new 3D-structured edge-coupling design promises higher-density fiber-to-chip optical input/output for co-packaged optics without competing with back-end metal routing."
---

Scaling optical input/output for co-packaged optics is limited by the fiber-to-chip interface, where conventional edge couplers are confined to a single row along the chip facet and surface couplers are narrowband or hard to fabricate. The authors introduce volumetric edge coupling, in which the chip edge is structured in three dimensions so that optical coupling can occur over a two dimensional region rather than along a single line, aiming to interface multiple waveguides with multiple cores of a multicore fiber while keeping optical access from the chip perimeter. They study a single-channel design based on total-internal-reflection-mediated evanescent coupling through the 54.7 degree sidewall of a KOH-etched silicon cavity, with the coupling profile shaped by a wedged buried-oxide ridge. Finite-difference time-domain simulations predict a peak coupling efficiency of 88 percent (-0.56 dB) at 1550 nanometers and a 1 dB bandwidth of 86.45 nanometers spanning the C-band, along with a vertical alignment tolerance of about plus or minus 2 micrometers and a plus or minus 0.8 degree angular tolerance. The reflected optical field also offers a potential alignment signal that could reduce active alignment overhead in future multicore implementations.
