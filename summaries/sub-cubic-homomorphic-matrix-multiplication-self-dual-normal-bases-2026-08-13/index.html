Sub-Cubic Homomorphic Matrix Multiplication via Self-Dual Normal Bases

IACR ePrint 2026/1592 · full view ↗

Overview and Claim

This IACR ePrint preprint (Paper 2026/1592, received 2026-08-03, revised 2026-08-06, category "Public-key cryptography", CC BY) studies how to embed the matrix algebra $M_N(\mathbb{F}_p)$ into a commutative cyclotomic ring so that one homomorphic ciphertext-ciphertext multiplication, followed by a Galois trace, yields the matrix product. Authors: Efe İzbudak (Department of Mathematics, Middle East Technical University, Ankara), Kübra Kaytancı, Ferruh Özbudak and Erkay Savaş (Faculty of Engineering and Natural Sciences, Sabancı University, Istanbul).

The starting point is the coprime tensor decomposition of Zheng, Li and Wang (ZLW, Designs Codes and Cryptography 93, 2025), which packs an $N \times N$ matrix product into a single BGV ciphertext multiplication plus $O(\log N)$ automorphisms, but only for compact matrices $N \le n^{1/3}$ where $n$ is the ring degree, and at the cost of two plaintext-ciphertext multiplications (CMult) for homomorphic basis switching. The paper makes four moves:

  1. A lower bound. Via the Cohn-Umans group-algebra framework, a single-multiplication bilinear monomial embedding into a commutative cyclotomic ring forces the Abelian Triple Product Property (TPP) on the exponent group, hence ring degree $n = \widetilde{\Omega}(N^3)$ (Theorem 1). Escaping to non-commutative (skew) algebras is shown impossible under BGV (Lemma 2, Theorem 3).
  2. Beating the bound. Routing the 7 Strassen sub-products through 7 distinct prime ideals (CRT orthogonal idempotents) of the unramified cyclotomic extension relaxes the requirement to $n = \widetilde{\Omega}(N^{\log_2 7}) \approx \widetilde{\Omega}(N^{2.81})$ (Theorem 4).
  3. Normal bases instead of power bases. Embedding the inner tensor factor into the maximal real subfield $K^{+} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_2} + \zeta_{m_2}^{-1})$ makes the extension degree $s' = \varphi(m_2)/2$, which for suitably chosen moduli $m_2$ (for example $m_2 \in \{7, 9, 14, 18\}$, giving $s' = 3$) is odd, satisfying the Lempel-Weinberger parity condition and yielding a Self-Dual Normal Basis (SDNB) with $\gamma_0 = \theta$. One generator replaces $O(s)$ stored polynomials, CMult drops to 0, noise becomes symmetric between operands, and the trace automorphism depth is structurally halved.
  4. Boundless matrices. Deferring the trace to after the Strassen recombination lets block-Strassen run directly over ciphertexts with no homomorphic basis switching, giving $O(N^{\log_2 7 - 1/\rho})$ Mult and $\widetilde{O}(N^{2 - 2/(\rho \log_2 7)})$ Aut for $N = n^{\rho}$ (Theorem 17).

Implementation over HElib/BGV is public at https://github.com/ephemer4l/NBMM. Note the abstract's headline pairs numbers from two different Table 3 rows; the exact attributions are given in the Experimental Results section below.

Evaluation Pipeline

The end-to-end procedure is the commutative diagram of Figure 1: encode both plaintext matrices into $\overline{R}$ under the SDNB, encrypt to BGV ciphertexts in $R_q^2$, perform one homomorphic multiplication (with relinearization) to obtain $c_{\mathrm{mult}} \in R_q^2$, apply the towered Galois trace to reach $c_{\mathrm{trace}} \in R_{q''}^2$, decrypt to $P_{\mathrm{out}} \in \overline{R}_{13}$, and decode the orthogonal idempotents back to $AB \in M_N(\mathbb{F}_p)$. The diagram commutes with the plaintext path (encode, matrix product, decode).

Prior Work and the Asymptotic Landscape

Naive entry-wise encoding needs $N^2$ ciphertexts per matrix. Halevi and Shoup's HElib matrix-vector algorithms, extended to matrix-matrix products by Lu et al. and Wang and Huang, cut the representation to $O(N)$ ciphertexts but require $O(N^2)$ CMult, $O(N^2)$ Mult and $O(N^2 \log N)$ Aut. Single-ciphertext diagonal packing (Cheon et al.; Rathee et al.) reduces this to $O(N)$ Mult and $O(N \log N)$ Aut; routing optimizations (Jiang et al.; Jang et al.) bring Aut to $O(N)$; Rizomiliotis and Triakosia reduce Mult to 1 while still needing $O(N)$ sequential automorphisms. ZLW replaced SIMD batching with the coprime tensor decomposition.

Four limitations of the ZLW power-basis formulation motivate the paper. First, homomorphic basis switching costs two CMult before the Mult, raising depth and noise. Second, the trace dual of a power basis has index-dependent canonical norms, so the right operand's error is inflated and a larger ciphertext modulus is needed. Third, $O(N)$ distinct polynomial generators must be computed and stored before encryption. Fourth, embedding into a single global tensor ring imposes the $\widetilde{\Omega}(N^3)$ TPP degree floor absent spatial ideal decomposition.

Algebraic Preliminaries

Cyclotomics and Galois theory. For $\zeta_m = e^{2\pi i / m}$, the field $K = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m)$ is abelian over $\mathbb{Q}$ of degree $n = \varphi(m)$, with $\mathrm{Gal}(K/\mathbb{Q}) \cong (\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$ and each automorphism $\tau_k(\zeta_m) = \zeta_m^k$ for $\gcd(k,m) = 1$. The maximal real subfield is $K^{+} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m + \zeta_m^{-1})$ with $[K^{+} : \mathbb{Q}] = \varphi(m)/2$. The ring of integers $\mathcal{O}_K = \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_m] \cong R = \mathbb{Z}[x]/\langle \Phi_m(x) \rangle$. For a rational prime $p \nmid m$ the ideal $p\mathcal{O}_K$ is unramified and splits as $\mathfrak{p}_1 \mathfrak{p}_2 \cdots \mathfrak{p}_{\ell}$ with $\ell = \varphi(m)/d$ and $d = \mathrm{ord}_m(p)$. The decomposition group $D_{\mathfrak{p}_i} = \{\sigma : \sigma(\mathfrak{p}_i) = \mathfrak{p}_i\}$ is cyclic of order $d$, generated by the Frobenius $\sigma_p$ with $\sigma_p(\alpha) \equiv \alpha^p \pmod{\mathfrak{p}_i}$, and $\mathcal{O}_K / \mathfrak{p}_i \cong \mathbb{F}_{p^d}$.

Tensor decomposition. For pairwise coprime $m = m_1 m_2 \cdots m_k$,

$$\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_m) \cong \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_1}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_2}) \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \cdots \otimes_{\mathbb{Q}} \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_k}),$$

and because the discriminants are pairwise coprime the rings of integers factor likewise, $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_m] \cong \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m_1}] \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \cdots \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} \mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{m_k}]$.

Canonical embedding and norms. The canonical embedding $\sigma : K \hookrightarrow \mathbb{C}^n$ sends $a$ to its evaluations at all primitive roots, $\sigma(a) = \left( a(\zeta_m^k) \right)_{\gcd(k,m)=1}$, and the canonical norm is

$$\lVert a \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} = \lVert \sigma(a) \rVert_{\infty} = \max_{\gcd(k,m)=1} \left| a(\zeta_m^k) \right|,$$

which is sub-multiplicative, $\lVert ab \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} \le \lVert a \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} \lVert b \rVert^{\mathrm{can}}$. The coefficient norm obeys $\lVert a \rVert_{\infty} \le c_m \lVert a \rVert^{\mathrm{can}}$ with $c_m$ the cyclotomic expansion factor.

Tensor rank. A bilinear map $T : U \times V \to W$ is a tensor $T \in U^{*} \otimes_F V^{*} \otimes_F W$; its rank is the least $r$ with $T = \sum_{i=1}^{r} \phi_i \otimes \psi_i \otimes w_i$, and this lower-bounds the non-scalar multiplications needed. For matrix multiplication $\mathrm{rk}(T_{\mathrm{mat}}) \ge 2N^2 - 1$.

Discrete Gaussians. With $\rho_{\varsigma}(x) = \exp(-\pi x^2 / \varsigma^2)$,

$$D_{\mathbb{Z},\varsigma}(x) = \frac{\exp(-\pi x^2 / \varsigma^2)}{\sum_{z \in \mathbb{Z}} \exp(-\pi z^2 / \varsigma^2)},$$

which is sub-Gaussian with $\Pr[|X| \ge t] \le 2\exp(-\pi t^2 / \varsigma^2)$. Sampling the $n$ coefficients of an error polynomial $e$ independently gives $\lVert e \rVert_{\infty}, \lVert e \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} \le C \varsigma \sqrt{n}$ with probability at least $1 - 2^{-\Omega(n)}$.

Trace pairings and normal bases. For a separable extension $E/F$ of degree $s$, $\mathrm{Tr}_{E/F}(\alpha) = \sum_{\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/F)} \sigma(\alpha)$ induces a symmetric non-degenerate form $\langle x, y \rangle = \mathrm{Tr}_{E/F}(xy)$; every basis $B = \{v_0,\dots,v_{s-1}\}$ has a unique dual $B^{\vee}$ with $\mathrm{Tr}_{E/F}(v_i v_j^{\vee}) = \delta_{i,j}$. A normal basis is $\mathcal{N} = \{\sigma(\theta) : \sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/F)\}$; its trace dual is also normal, with generator $\gamma_0$. A Type I Optimal Normal Basis exists when $s + 1$ is prime and $p$ generates $(\mathbb{Z}/(s+1)\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$.

Galois rings. If $f(x) \in \mathbb{Z}_{p^r}[x]$ is monic of degree $s$ with irreducible reduction mod $p$, then $\mathrm{GR}(p^r, s) = \mathbb{Z}_{p^r}[x]/\langle f(x) \rangle$ is a local ring with maximal ideal $p\,\mathrm{GR}(p^r,s)$ and residue field $\mathbb{F}_{p^s}$.

RLWE and BGV. With $R_q = \mathbb{Z}_q[x]/\langle \Phi_m(x) \rangle$, RLWE samples are $(a, as + e)$ for uniform $a$ and $e \leftarrow \chi$; decisional $\mathrm{RLWE}_{n,q,\chi}$ asserts indistinguishability from uniform. A BGV ciphertext is $c = (c_0, c_1) \in R_q^2$ decrypting via $[[c_0 + c_1 s]_q]_p = m$, with $c_0 + c_1 s = m + pe$ in $R_q$. Multiplication tensors to $(d_0, d_1, d_2) = (c_0 c_0', c_0 c_1' + c_1 c_0', c_1 c_1')$ under basis $(1, s, s^2)$ and needs relinearization; modulus switching $q \to q'$ adds rounding error bounded by $B_{\mathrm{scale}}$. Applying $\tau_k(x) = x^k$ permutes the plaintext to $m(x^k)$ under key $s(x^k)$, so a key switch is required: $c_1(x^k)$ is split by a digit-extraction map $\mathcal{D}_B : R_q \to R_q^{D+1}$ into base-$B$ digits with $D = \lfloor \log_B q \rfloor$, and the inner product against $\mathrm{evk}_k \in M_{(D+1) \times 2}(R_q)$ injects additive error bounded by

$$B_{\mathrm{ks}} = 16\sqrt{3}\, p\, n\, \varsigma\, \lceil \log_B q \rceil.$$

The Coprime Tensor Decomposition (Baseline)

Let $R = \mathbb{Z}[x]/\langle \Phi_m(x) \rangle$ with $m = m_1 m_2 m_3$ pairwise coprime, so $R \cong R_1 \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} R_2 \otimes_{\mathbb{Z}} R_3$ with $R_i = \mathbb{Z}[x]/\langle \Phi_{m_i}(x) \rangle$; reducing mod $p$ gives the $\mathbb{F}_p$-algebra isomorphism $\overline{R} \cong \overline{R}_1 \otimes_{\mathbb{F}_p} \overline{R}_2 \otimes_{\mathbb{F}_p} \overline{R}_3$. Write $r = \varphi(m_1)$, $s = \varphi(m_2)$, $t = \varphi(m_3)$. For $A \in M_{r \times s}(\mathbb{F}_p)$ and $B \in M_{s \times t}(\mathbb{F}_p)$, ZLW encode

$$m_A = \sum_{i=0}^{r-1} \sum_{j=0}^{s-1} a_{ij} \left( u_i \otimes v_j \otimes 1 \right), \qquad m_B = \sum_{k=0}^{s-1} \sum_{l=0}^{t-1} b_{kl} \left( 1 \otimes v_k^{\vee} \otimes w_l \right),$$

where $\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}_2/\mathbb{F}_p}(v_j v_k^{\vee}) = \delta_{j,k}$. The product is

$$m_A m_B = \sum_{i=0}^{r-1} \sum_{j=0}^{s-1} \sum_{k=0}^{s-1} \sum_{l=0}^{t-1} a_{ij} b_{kl} \left( u_i \otimes (v_j v_k^{\vee}) \otimes w_l \right),$$

and applying the trace to $\overline{R}_{13} = \mathbb{F}_p[x]/\langle \Phi_{m_1 m_3}(x) \rangle$, which is the identity on $R_1$ and $R_3$, collapses the inner pairing:

$$\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}/\overline{R}_{13}}(m_A m_B) = \sum_{i=0}^{r-1} \sum_{l=0}^{t-1} \left( \sum_{j=0}^{s-1} a_{ij} b_{jl} \right) (u_i \otimes 1 \otimes w_l),$$

isolating $AB$ over $R_1 \otimes_{\mathbb{F}_p} R_3$. Two limitations follow from using the power basis $v_j = x^j$. (i) Asymmetric norm expansion: the duals $v_j^{\vee}$ expand as polynomial fractions in the inverse formal derivative $\Phi'_{m_2}(x)^{-1}$, so $\max_j \lVert v_j^{\vee} \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} > \lVert v_j \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} = 1$, magnifying the right operand's error and forcing a larger modulus. (ii) Generator storage: $2s$ distinct basis polynomials must be computed and stored before encryption.

Lower Bound: Monomial Embeddings Need $\widetilde{\Omega}(N^3)$

Definition 1. A bilinear polynomial embedding of $M_N(\mathbb{F}_p)$ over $\overline{R}$ is a triplet $(K, D, n)$: $K$ is the rank of the free module $\overline{R}^K$ needed to encode one matrix, $D$ the tensor degree of the evaluation polynomial, and $n = \varphi(m)$ the ring dimension over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

Theorem 1. $\Psi : M_N(\mathbb{F}_p) \to \overline{R}^K$ must be an $\mathbb{F}_p$-linear injection, so $K \cdot n \ge \dim_{\mathbb{F}_p} M_N(\mathbb{F}_p) = N^2$. A bilinear monomial embedding in a single commutative ring ($K = 1$) evaluated with one multiplication ($D = 1$) requires $n = \widetilde{\Omega}(N^3)$.

Proof sketch. View $\overline{R} \cong \mathbb{F}_p[x]/\langle \Phi_m(x) \rangle$ as a quotient of the group algebra $\mathbb{F}_p[G]$ for $G = \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$. The argument rests on an explicit hypothesis: the encoded monomials are assumed to map strictly into the cyclotomic power basis $\{1, x, \dots, x^{\varphi(m)-1}\}$, so that they remain linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_p$ and the polynomial convolution mirrors the group algebra exactly, with no modular folding. Under that assumption, encode $P_A(x) = \sum_{i,j} A_{ij} x^{f(i,j)}$, $P_B(x) = \sum_{k,l} B_{kl} x^{g(k,l)}$ with index maps $f, g : \{1,\dots,N\}^2 \to G$. Then

$$P_A(x) P_B(x) = \sum_{i,j,k,l=1}^{N} A_{ij} B_{kl} \, x^{f(i,j) + g(k,l)}.$$

Extracting $C_{il} = \sum_j A_{ij} B_{jl}$ from the coefficient of $x^{c_{il}}$ for all inputs demands the global non-interference condition

$$f(i,j) + g(k,l) = c_{i'l'} \iff i = i', \; l = l', \; j = k.$$

The alignment case $j = k$ makes $f(i,j) + g(j,l)$ independent of $j$, forcing $f(i,j) - f(i,k) = g(k,l) - g(j,l) = h(j,k)$. Fixing $k = 1$ and setting $S_i = f(i,1)$, $T_j = -h(j,1)$ yields $f(i,j) = S_i - T_j$; the same argument on the other side gives $g(j,l) = T_j - U_l$ and $c_{il} = S_i - U_l$. Substituting back,

$$(S_i - T_j) + (T_k - U_l) = S_{i'} - U_{l'} \iff (S_i - S_{i'}) + (T_k - T_j) + (U_{l'} - U_l) = 0,$$

which is exactly the Abelian Triple Product Property for $S, T, U \subseteq G$. Distinctness of the $N^2$ targets $c_{il}$ forces $|S| = |U| = N$, and $T_k - T_j = 0 \Rightarrow j = k$ forces $|T| = N$. By the Cohn-Umans theorem, TPP implies $|G| \ge |S||T||U| = N^3$, so $m \ge N^3$. Since $\varphi(m) = \Omega(m / \log\log m)$, we get $n = \widetilde{\Omega}(N^3)$.

Lemma 2 (no skew trace functional). For a finite Galois $E/F$ and non-trivial $\sigma \in \mathrm{Gal}(E/F)$, there is no $F$-linear $T : E \to F$ with $T(xy) = \mathrm{Tr}_{E/F}(x \sigma(y))$ for all $x, y$. Indeed $T(1 \cdot (xy)) = \mathrm{Tr}_{E/F}(\sigma(xy)) = \mathrm{Tr}_{E/F}(xy)$ by Galois invariance, so $\mathrm{Tr}_{E/F}(x(\sigma(y) - y)) = 0$ for all $x$; non-degeneracy gives $\sigma(y) = y$ for all $y$, contradicting non-triviality.

Theorem 3 (skew algebras are incompatible). A central simple algebra such as the skew polynomial ring $E[Y; \sigma]/\langle Y^N - 1 \rangle \cong M_N(F)$ cannot be embedded into a commutative quotient ring so that one tensor product computes the matrix product. Commutative convolution of $\psi(A) = \sum A_{ij}\theta_i Y^j$ produces cross-terms $A_{ij} B_{kl} \theta_i \theta_k Y^{j+l}$, whereas the skew rule needs $A_{ij} B_{kl} \theta_i \sigma^j(\theta_k) Y^{j+l}$. By Lemma 2 no post-multiplication linear trace can convert $xy$ into $x\sigma^j(y)$, so $O(N)$ sequential automorphisms would be needed before the multiplication, violating $D = 1$.

Beating the Bound: CRT-Strassen Hybrids

Theorem 4. Partitioning $M_N(\mathbb{F}_p)$ into blocks and mapping the sub-tensors into distinct prime ideals of the unramified cyclotomic extension relaxes the single-multiplication degree requirement to $n = \widetilde{\Omega}(N^{\log_2 7})$.

Proof sketch. Let $p\mathcal{O}_K$ split into $\ell$ primes, so by CRT $\overline{R} \cong \bigoplus_{v=1}^{\ell} \mathbb{F}_{p^d}$ with $d = \mathrm{ord}_m(p)$. Write $N = 2^k N_b$ for a constant base block size $N_b > 1$. Each Strassen halving needs 7 products of linear combinations of sub-matrices; the pre-multiplication linear combinations are computed in the plaintext encoding phase, and the 7 independent products are routed into 7 distinct prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}_v$ using the CRT primitive orthogonal idempotents $e_v$. Inside each local slot the evaluation reduces modulo a degree-$d$ irreducible, so satisfying the Cohn-Umans TPP bound locally (preventing exponent aliasing) requires $d = \widetilde{\Omega}(N_b^3)$. Slot survival through the trace is guaranteed by Theorem 15. Since $n = \ell d$, provisioning $\ell \ge 7$ gives $n \ge 7 \cdot \widetilde{\Omega}(N_b^3)$; recursing to the base block gives $7^{\log_2(N/N_b)} = O(N^{\log_2 7})$ independent parallel multiplications, and the normal-basis TPP trace is applied only at dimension $N_b$. Supporting $\ell = O(N^{\log_2 7})$ slots for fixed $p$ needs $d = \Omega(\log N)$. Because the intermediate Strassen products sit in orthogonal slots, recombining them homomorphically would need expensive rotations, so the post-multiplication Strassen additions are deferred to client-side plaintext decoding. Overall

$$n = \ell d \ge O(N^{\log_2 7}) \cdot \max\!\left( \widetilde{\Omega}(N_b^3), \; \Omega(\log N) \right) \;\Longrightarrow\; n = \widetilde{\Omega}(N^{\log_2 7}) \approx \widetilde{\Omega}(N^{2.81}).$$

Normal Basis Embeddings and the Parity Constraint

The extension $\mathbb{F}_{p^s}/\mathbb{F}_p$ admits a generator $\theta$ and trace dual generator $\gamma_0$ with $\mathcal{N} = \{\sigma^j(\theta)\}$, $\mathcal{N}^{\vee} = \{\sigma^j(\gamma_0)\}$ and orthogonality

$$\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}_2/\mathbb{F}_p}\!\left( \sigma^i(\theta) \sigma^j(\gamma_0) \right) = \delta_{i,j}.$$

Remark 1 (Lempel-Weinberger parity constraint). A finite extension $\mathbb{F}_{p^s}/\mathbb{F}_p$ of odd characteristic admits a self-dual normal basis ($\gamma_0 = \theta$) if and only if $s$ is odd. Here $s = \varphi(m_2)$, and $\varphi(m_2)$ is even for all $m_2 \ge 3$, so no SDNB exists over $\overline{R}_2$. The paper explicitly rejects the previously proposed workaround of zero-padding an $s \times s$ matrix into an $(s+1) \times (s+1)$ module: that conflates vector space dimension with the algebraic degree $s = [\overline{R}_2 : \mathbb{F}_p]$. Padding changes the rank of the encoded data vectors in $M_{s+1}(\mathbb{F}_p)$, not the field degree; the Galois group order stays even and the discriminant of the trace form still forbids an orthogonal normal basis over $\mathbb{F}_p$.

Lemma 5 (dual generator via circulant Gram inversion). For even $s$ a distinct $\gamma_0 \ne \theta$ is required and is obtained by inverting an $s \times s$ symmetric circulant Gram matrix over $\mathbb{F}_p$. With $M_{i,j} = \mathrm{Tr}(\sigma^i(\theta)\sigma^j(\theta))$, Galois invariance gives $M_{i,j} = \mathrm{Tr}(\theta \sigma^{j-i}(\theta))$, so $M$ depends only on $(j - i) \bmod s$ (circulant) and is symmetric by commutativity; non-degeneracy of the trace pairing makes $M$ non-singular. Taking $(c_0,\dots,c_{s-1})$ as the $0$-th row of $M^{-1}$ and $\gamma_0 = \sum_k c_k \sigma^k(\theta)$ gives $\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma^i(\theta)\gamma_0) = (M^{-1}M)_{0,i} = \delta_{0,i}$, and applying $\sigma^{-j}$ yields $\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma^i(\theta)\sigma^j(\gamma_0)) = \delta_{i,j}$.

Theorem 6 (SDNB via the maximal real subfield). Let $\overline{R}_2^{+} = \mathbb{Z}[x]/\langle \Psi_{m_2}(x) \rangle \bmod p$ be the algebra of $K^{+} = \mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{m_2} + \zeta_{m_2}^{-1})$, of degree $s' = \varphi(m_2)/2$. For suitable moduli, for example $m_2 \in \{7, 9, 14, 18\}$ giving $s' = 3$, the degree $s'$ is odd. Assuming $p$ generates $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{+}/\mathbb{Q})$, the ideal $(p)$ is inert in $\mathcal{O}_{K^{+}}$, so $\Psi_{m_2}(x)$ is irreducible mod $p$ and $\overline{R}_2^{+} \cong \mathbb{F}_{p^{s'}}$. By Lempel-Weinberger (odd $s'$ in odd characteristic; $s' \not\equiv 0 \bmod 4$ for $p = 2$) an SDNB exists: $\mathcal{N} = \{\sigma^i(\theta) : 0 \le i < s'\}$ with $\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}_2^{+}/\mathbb{F}_p}(\sigma^i(\theta)\sigma^j(\theta)) = \delta_{i,j}$. Since $\gamma_0 = \theta$, both operands use one generator, halving pre-computation storage and uniformizing canonical noise bounds.

Remark 2 (halved trace automorphism depth). Elements of $K^{+}$ are invariant under complex conjugation, so applying the full $\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}_2/\mathbb{F}_p}$ ($s$ automorphisms) would compute the subfield trace twice and introduce a scalar $2 \bmod p$. Restricting the homomorphic extraction to the $s' = s/2$ automorphisms of $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{+}/\mathbb{Q})$ maps $\overline{R}_2^{+}$ directly to $\mathbb{F}_p$, produces exactly the $\delta_{i,j}$ components with no scalar correction, and structurally halves the automorphism depth.

Theorem 7 (Type I ONB fallback). If the maximal real subfield is unavailable and $\overline{R}_2$ admits a Type I ONB ($s + 1$ prime, $p$ generating $(\mathbb{Z}/(s+1)\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$), then the integer lift of the dual generator satisfies

$$\lVert \tilde{\gamma}_0 \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} \le |C| \, \lVert \tilde{\theta} \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} + |C|,$$

where $C \in [-(p-1)/2, (p-1)/2]$ is the symmetric integer representative of $(s+1)^{-1} \bmod p$. Proof sketch. The basis elements are the $s$ non-trivial roots of $x^{s+1} - 1$; since $p$ generates the unit group there is $j = s/2$ with $p^{j} \equiv -1 \pmod{s+1}$, so $\sigma^{s/2}(\theta) = \theta^{-1}$ and $\theta \sigma^{s/2}(\theta) = 1$ with $\mathrm{Tr}(1) = s \bmod p$; all other cross-terms are primitive $(s+1)$-th roots of unity whose trace is $-1 \bmod p$ (the roots sum to zero). Positing $\gamma_0 = c\,\sigma^{s/2}(\theta) + d$ and imposing $\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma^k(\theta)\gamma_0) = \delta_{k,0}$ gives $d = -c$ from $k \ne 0$ and $c(s+1) \equiv 1 \pmod p$ from $k = 0$, so

$$\gamma_0 = (s+1)^{-1} \sigma^{s/2}(\theta) - (s+1)^{-1}.$$

Lifting, $\sigma^{s/2}$ becomes $x \mapsto x^{p^{s/2}}$, which permutes primitive $m$-th roots of unity bijectively (as $\gcd(p,m) = 1$) and is therefore a canonical isometry, $\lVert \tilde{\theta}(x^{p^{s/2}}) \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} = \lVert \tilde{\theta} \rVert^{\mathrm{can}}$. Setting $\tilde{\gamma}_0(x) = C\tilde{\theta}(x^{p^{s/2}}) - C$ and applying the triangle inequality gives the bound: an affine shift instead of the fractional variance of a general Gram inversion over $\mathbb{Z}[x]$.

Trace Extraction of the Matrix Product

Theorem 8. With $\mathcal{N}$ and its trace dual $\mathcal{N}^{\vee}$,

$$\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}/\overline{R}_{13}}(P_A P_B) = \sum_{i,k} c_{ik} \left( u_i \otimes 1 \otimes w_k \right), \qquad c_{ik} = \sum_{j=0}^{s-1} a_{ij} b_{jk}.$$

Proof sketch. The trace factors as $\mathrm{id}_{R_1} \otimes \mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}_2/\mathbb{F}_p} \otimes \mathrm{id}_{R_3}$, and

$$P_A P_B = \sum_{i=0}^{r-1} \sum_{k=0}^{t-1} \sum_{j=0}^{s-1} \sum_{l=0}^{s-1} a_{ij} b_{lk} \left( u_i \otimes \left( \sigma^j(\theta) \sigma^l(\gamma_0) \right) \otimes w_k \right).$$

For standard normal bases the trace evaluates $s$ automorphisms over $\overline{R}_2$; for SDNB encodings in $K^{+}$ it evaluates only the $s' = s/2$ automorphisms of $\mathrm{Gal}(K^{+}/\mathbb{Q})$, bypassing the duplication scalar $2 \bmod p$ (Remark 2). Galois invariance lets us apply $\sigma^{-l}$, giving $\mathrm{Tr}(\sigma^j(\theta)\sigma^l(\gamma_0)) = \mathrm{Tr}(\sigma^{j-l}(\theta)\gamma_0) = \delta_{j - l \bmod s, 0}$, so the double sum collapses to $j = l$ and recovers $c_{ik}$.

Norm Uniformity and Noise Bounds

Under power bases the primary basis has constant norm $\lVert v_j \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} = 1$ but the duals have index-dependent norms, and the encoding error for $B$ is dominated by $E_{\mathrm{dual}} = \max_j \lVert v_j^{\vee} \rVert^{\mathrm{can}}$. The canonical norm of an algebraic integer is Frobenius-invariant, but that symmetry is lost if the integer coefficients are first reduced into the symmetric interval $[-(p-1)/2, (p-1)/2]$: modular reduction over $\mathbb{Z}$ changes the polynomial and destroys the invariance over $\mathbb{C}$. The fix is to evaluate canonical integer lifts over $\mathbb{Z}$, without coefficient-wise reduction mod $p$.

Theorem 9. For canonical integer lifts $\tilde{\theta}, \tilde{\gamma}_0 \in \mathbb{Z}[x]/\langle \Phi_m(x) \rangle$ and all $0 \le j \le s-1$,

$$\lVert \tilde{\theta}(x^{p^j}) \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} = \lVert \tilde{\theta} \rVert^{\mathrm{can}}, \qquad \lVert \tilde{\gamma}_0(x^{p^j}) \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} = \lVert \tilde{\gamma}_0 \rVert^{\mathrm{can}}.$$

Proof. $\lVert \tilde{\theta}(x^{p^j}) \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} = \max_{\gcd(k,m)=1} |\tilde{\theta}(\zeta_m^{k p^j})|$, and since $\gcd(p,m) = 1$ the map $k \mapsto k p^j \bmod m$ permutes $(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}$, so the set of evaluation points is unchanged and the maximum is invariant. The same holds for $\tilde{\gamma}_0$.

Lemma 10 (final error bound). Let $c_A, c_B \in R_q^2$ encrypt $P_A, P_B$ with initial error norms $N_A, N_B$, and let the uniform plaintext basis bounds be $\lVert \sigma^j(\tilde{\theta}) \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} \le E_{\theta}$ and $\lVert \sigma^j(\tilde{\gamma}_0) \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} \le E_{\gamma}$. Then the trace output encrypting $AB$ has error bounded by

$$E_{\mathrm{final}} \le s\left( E_{\mathrm{mult}} + B_{\mathrm{ks}} \right) + (s-1) B_{\mathrm{ks}}, \qquad E_{\mathrm{mult}} = \lVert P_A \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} N_B + \lVert P_B \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} N_A + p N_A N_B.$$

Proof sketch. Sub-multiplicativity of the canonical norm gives $E_{\mathrm{mult}}$ for the degree-2 ciphertext; relinearization adds $B_{\mathrm{ks}}$, so the ciphertext entering the trace has error at most $E_{\mathrm{mult}} + B_{\mathrm{ks}}$. The trace sums $\sum_{i=0}^{s-1} \sigma^i(c)$, and by Theorem 9 the base error scales symmetrically by $s$. The binary evaluation tree of Algorithm 2 adds key-switching error obeying $N_{2h} \le 2N_h + B_{\mathrm{ks}}$ (each halving step shifts an $h$-term sum, costing one key switch) and $N_{2h+1} \le N_{2h} + B_{\mathrm{ks}}$ (odd residual), with $N_1 = 0$; resolving over $s$ terms gives $(s-1)B_{\mathrm{ks}}$. Critically, $E_{\gamma}$ is bounded by the single norm $\lVert \tilde{\gamma}_0 \rVert^{\mathrm{can}}$, which is $O(1)$ under an ONB or equal to $E_{\theta}$ under an SDNB, rather than by a supremum over a divergent set.

Lemma 11 (Kronecker). A non-zero algebraic integer all of whose Galois conjugates have absolute value at most 1 is a root of unity.

Theorem 12 (power basis is optimal outside the trace). The power basis minimizes the maximum canonical norm among all full-rank integral bases for the non-extracted components $R_1$ and $R_3$. Proof. For $\mathcal{P} = \{1, x, \dots, x^{\varphi(m_i)-1}\}$ every element has $\lVert x^k \rVert^{\mathrm{can}} = 1$. For an arbitrary full-rank integral basis $U$, each $u_j \ne 0$ maps to a non-zero algebraic integer $u_j(\zeta_{m_i})$, and $\lVert u_j \rVert^{\mathrm{can}}$ is the maximum absolute value over its Galois conjugates. By Lemma 11 that maximum is at least 1 (either $u_j(\zeta_{m_i})$ is a root of unity, or some conjugate exceeds 1). Hence no integral basis beats the uniform value 1 attained by $\mathcal{P}$. Since dual inversion is not needed on $R_1$ and $R_3$, the power basis is retained there.

Logarithmic and Towered Trace Evaluation

A direct trace costs $O(s)$ sequential additions and automorphisms. Define the partial sum $S_k(c) = \sum_{i=0}^{k-1} \sigma^i(c)$ and recurse $S_{2h}(c) = S_h(c) + \sigma^h(S_h(c))$, with an extra residual $S_{2h+1}(c) = S_{2h}(c) + \sigma^{2h}(c)$ for odd term counts. The key observation is that base-$B$ digit decomposition commutes with $\sigma^h$: if $a(x) = \sum_m d^{(m)}(x) B^m$ then $\sigma^h(a(x)) = \sum_m d^{(m)}(x^h) B^m$ in $R_q$. Reducing the shifted digits modulo $\Phi_m(x)$ over $\mathbb{Z}$ inflates their coefficient infinity norms, but $\sigma^h$ permutes the roots of unity and is therefore a canonical-embedding isometry; since BGV correctness and noise bounds depend on the canonical norm, the automorphism can be applied directly to already-extracted digits without re-decomposing the reduced shifted polynomials.

Theorem 13. Algorithm 2 evaluates the Galois trace with $\lfloor \log_2 s \rfloor$ applications of $\mathcal{D}_B$ and $\lfloor \log_2 s \rfloor + \mathrm{hw}(s) - 1$ key-switching operations, where $\mathrm{hw}(s)$ is the Hamming weight of $s$; applying the automorphism to the extracted digits before the inner product preserves the additive bound $B_{\mathrm{ks}}$. Proof sketch. $\mathcal{D}_B$ is applied only to the masking polynomial $c_1$; the initial decomposition $c_1 = \sum_{m=0}^{D} c_1^{(m)} B^m$ is cached once and reused for the odd residuals and the base case $h = 1$. Each halving step with $h > 1$ decomposes the new masking polynomial $c_{\mathrm{half},1}$, giving $\lfloor \log_2 s \rfloor - 1$ recursive decompositions plus the cached one. The halving steps execute $\lfloor \log_2 s \rfloor$ key-switch inner products; the odd residual steps, which reuse the cached digits, contribute $\mathrm{hw}(s) - 1$.

Theorem 14 (towered trace). For composite $s = d_1 d_2$, evaluating

$$\mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^s}/\mathbb{F}_p} = \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^{d_1}}/\mathbb{F}_p} \circ \mathrm{Tr}_{\mathbb{F}_{p^s}/\mathbb{F}_{p^{d_1}}}$$

with an intermediate modulus switch reduces the additive key-switching noise from $O(s) B_{\mathrm{ks}}$ to $O(d_1 + d_2) B_{\mathrm{ks}}$. Proof sketch. The inner trace evaluates $d_2$ automorphisms over the subgroup generated by $\sigma^{d_1}$, accumulating $O(d_2) B_{\mathrm{ks}}$. Without a modulus switch the outer trace over $d_1$ automorphisms scales that error by $d_1$, totalling $O(d_1 d_2) B_{\mathrm{ks}} = O(s) B_{\mathrm{ks}}$. Pausing after the inner trace and switching $q \to q'$ divides the accumulated $O(d_2) B_{\mathrm{ks}}$ by $q/q'$, absorbing it into rounding noise; the outer trace then accumulates a fresh, independent $O(d_1) B_{\mathrm{ks}}$. For a fully factored $s$ this recursion drives $O(s)$ down to $O(\log s)$, permitting a smaller initial modulus $Q$.

Idempotent Stability for Parallel Slots

The global ring dimension exceeds the $N^2$ entries of one matrix, so by Artin-Wedderburn $\overline{R} = R/pR$ decomposes into a direct sum of finite fields, supporting $\ell$ parallel matrix products in distinct prime ideals. But trace automorphisms over an arbitrary ideal decomposition can destroy orthogonality of the block idempotents. The extraction trace applies $\tau_k(x) = x^k$ with $k \equiv 1 \pmod{m_1 m_3}$.

Theorem 15. Let $\overline{R} \cong \bigoplus_{v=1}^{\ell} \mathbb{F}_{p^d}$. The primitive orthogonal idempotents defining the parallel slots are preserved under Galois trace evaluations if and only if

$$\gcd\!\left( \mathrm{ord}_{m_2}(p), \; \mathrm{ord}_{m_1 m_3}(p) \right) = 1.$$

Proof sketch. Stabilizing $e_v$ requires $\tau_k \in D_{\mathfrak{p}_v}$, which is cyclic generated by $\sigma_p$. The trace automorphisms are defined by the simultaneous congruences $k \equiv p^j \pmod{m_2}$ and $k \equiv 1 \pmod{m_1 m_3}$ for $0 \le j < s$, $s = \mathrm{ord}_{m_2}(p)$. Requiring $\tau_k = \sigma_p^{c}$ means $p^c \equiv p^j \pmod{m_2}$ and $p^c \equiv 1 \pmod{m_1 m_3}$; with $t = \mathrm{ord}_{m_1 m_3}(p)$ this becomes

$$c \equiv j \pmod{s}, \qquad c \equiv 0 \pmod{t},$$

solvable iff $\gcd(s,t) \mid j$. Since this must hold for all $0 \le j < s$ and $s \ge 2$, taking $j = 1$ forces $\gcd(s,t) = 1$. Under that condition all $e_v$ are stabilized throughout the trace summation, isolating the subrings and preventing coefficient mixing across ideals.

Generalization to Galois Rings

Theorem 16. The normal basis bilinear embedding over $\mathbb{F}_{p^s}$ lifts to $\mathrm{GR}(p^r, s) \cong \mathbb{Z}_{p^r}[x]/\langle f(x) \rangle$, where $f$ is a basic irreducible factor of $\Phi_{m_2}(x)$ over $\mathbb{Z}_{p^r}$, supporting single-multiplication evaluation over $M_N(\mathbb{Z}_{p^r})$.

Proof sketch. Since $p \nmid m_2$, $\Phi_{m_2}(x)$ is separable mod $p$ and factors into distinct irreducibles of degree $s = \mathrm{ord}_{m_2}(p)$ over $\mathbb{F}_p$; by Hensel's Lemma an irreducible factor $\bar{f}$ lifts uniquely to monic $f \in \mathbb{Z}_{p^r}[x]$ dividing $\Phi_{m_2}(x)$, and $\mathrm{GR}(p^r,s)$ is a Galois ring with generalized Frobenius $\Sigma$ satisfying $\Sigma(x) \equiv x^p \pmod{p\,\mathrm{GR}(p^r,s)}$. If $\theta \in \mathbb{F}_{p^s}$ generates a normal basis and $\Theta$ is any lift, the elements $\Sigma^i(\Theta)$ reduce to an $\mathbb{F}_p$-basis mod $p$, hence form a free $\mathbb{Z}_{p^r}$-module basis by Nakayama's Lemma. The Gram matrix of $\{\Sigma^i(\Theta)\}$ reduces to a non-singular matrix over $\mathbb{F}_p$, and a matrix over $\mathbb{Z}_{p^r}$ with determinant coprime to $p$ is invertible, so a unique dual generator $\Gamma_0$ exists with $\mathrm{Tr}(\Sigma^i(\Theta)\Sigma^j(\Gamma_0)) = \delta_{i,j}$. Orthogonality therefore survives over $\mathbb{Z}_{p^r}$ and the tensor cross-terms collapse exactly as over the base field, giving high-precision integer arithmetic without modulo-$p$ overflow.

Boundless Matrix Dimensions

Practical RLWE implementations fix $n$ to powers of two (for example $n \in \{8192, 16384, 32768\}$) to optimize NTTs and standardize security parameters, so matrices with $N$ beyond the single-ciphertext capacity $N_{\max} = \widetilde{\Theta}(n^{1/\log_2 7})$ need a multi-ciphertext treatment. ZLW partition into $L \times L$ block matrices with $L = \lceil N/N_b \rceil$ and run Strassen over blocks, but their power bases force homomorphic basis switching on each of the $O(L^2)$ blocks, costing $O(L^2)$ CMult and $O(L^2 \log s)$ Aut purely as overhead.

Theorem 17. For fixed $n$ and $N = n^{\rho}$ with $\rho > 1/\log_2 7$, block-wise Strassen with SDNB/ONB encoding evaluates the product using exactly $0$ CMult, $O(N^{\log_2 7 - 1/\rho})$ Mult and $\widetilde{O}(N^{2 - 2/(\rho \log_2 7)})$ Aut.

Proof sketch. Partition $A, B$ into $L \times L$ blocks of size $N_{\max} \times N_{\max}$, padding to $L = 2^{\lceil \log_2 \lceil N/N_{\max} \rceil \rceil}$; padding at most doubles the grid, an $O(1)$ overhead. Every left block $A^{(i,j)}$ is encoded with the primary generator $\theta$ and every right block $B^{(i,j)}$ with the trace dual $\gamma_0$, yielding $2L^2$ ciphertexts already in the correct bilinear dual-basis configuration. Because Strassen combines $A$ blocks only with $A$ blocks and $B$ blocks only with $B$ blocks, the pre-encoded bases are invariant through the intermediate linear combinations: the scalar combinations distribute and the orthogonality $\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}_2/\mathbb{F}_p}(\sigma^i(\theta)\sigma^j(\gamma_0)) = \delta_{i,j}$ holds for the cross-products with no dynamic basis transformation, so CMult is exactly zero. Phase 2 performs $O(L^{\log_2 7})$ tensor multiplications (each one Mult with relinearization) and $O(L^{\log_2 7})$ homomorphic additions. Rather than tracing each of the $O(L^{\log_2 7})$ leaves, the $\mathbb{Z}$-linearity of the trace, $\mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}/\overline{R}_{13}}(\sum_k M_k) = \sum_k \mathrm{Tr}_{\overline{R}/\overline{R}_{13}}(M_k)$, lets the post-multiplication Strassen combinations run directly on the expanded tensor ciphertexts, condensing the grid to $L^2$ ciphertexts. Phase 3 applies the towered trace to each, costing $O(\log s)$ automorphisms per block (or $O(\log s')$ under SDNB), so $O(L^2 \log s)$ in total. Substituting $L = \Theta(N / n^{1/\log_2 7})$ and $n = N^{1/\rho}$:

$$\mathrm{Mult} = O\!\left( L^{\log_2 7} \right) = O\!\left( \frac{N^{\log_2 7}}{n} \right) = O\!\left( N^{\log_2 7 - \frac{1}{\rho}} \right),$$ $$\mathrm{Aut} = O\!\left( L^2 \log s \right) = \widetilde{O}\!\left( \frac{N^2}{n^{2/\log_2 7}} \right) = \widetilde{O}\!\left( N^{2 - \frac{2}{\rho \log_2 7}} \right).$$

With $\log_2 7 \approx 2.807$ this is $O(N^{2.807 - 1/\rho})$ Mult and $\widetilde{O}(N^{2 - 0.712/\rho})$ Aut. Since $1/\rho > \frac{\log_2 7}{3\rho} \approx 0.935/\rho$ and $\frac{2}{\rho \log_2 7} \approx 0.712/\rho > \frac{2}{3\rho} \approx 0.667/\rho$, the subtracted exponents are strictly larger than ZLW's $O(N^{2.807 - 0.935/\rho})$ Mult and $\widetilde{O}(N^{2 - 0.667/\rho})$ Aut, so both complexities are strictly smaller, while the $O(N^{2 - 0.667/\rho})$ CMult bottleneck is eliminated entirely.

Remark 3 (communication versus computation). Prior methods pack multiple blocks into one ciphertext to save bandwidth, which forces the $O(N^{2 - \frac{2}{3\rho}})$ CMult unpacking penalty. Since RLWE latency is usually dominated by high-depth algebraic operations rather than network transmission, transmitting the $O(L^2)$ block ciphertexts independently maximizes homomorphic throughput. Under strict bandwidth limits an outer-basis compression protocol could be added, trading the CMult $= 0$ advantage for compression.

Security

Remark 4 (distributional inequivalence of modulus embeddings). The paper rejects a common proof pattern in which a decisional RLWE challenge $(u, v)$ is embedded into a public-key ciphertext as $c = (u + m, v)$. That simulation does not reproduce the noise covariance of a real BGV encryption and is statistically distinguishable: an RLWE sample $(a, as + e)$ has a single error $e \leftarrow \chi$, whereas a BGV ciphertext is $c = (b_0 r + p e_0 + m, \; b_1 r + p e_1)$ with an ephemeral masking polynomial $r \leftarrow \chi$ multiplied against the public key $(b_0, b_1)$.

Because all matrix encodings in this paper are deterministic $\mathbb{Z}$-linear isomorphisms applied inside the plaintext algebra before encryption, they alter neither ciphertext structure nor RLWE error distributions. No lattice reduction is needed: semantic security of the matrix evaluation reduces directly to IND-CPA security of the underlying BGV scheme.

Combining the sub-cubic Strassen-CRT geometry, the $O(1)$ ONB dual expansion bound and the towered trace noise reduction shrinks the ciphertext modulus $Q$ needed for correct decryption relative to a plain power-basis parameterization (Table 2 of the paper):

In summary: bilinear monomial evaluations of the inner product are bounded by the Abelian Triple Product Property; the $\widetilde{\Omega}(N^3)$ limit is bypassed by routing CRT-Strassen hybrids through orthogonal ideal slots. Within the unramified cyclotomic extension, power bases are replaced by normal bases, with maximal real subfields circumventing the Lempel-Weinberger parity constraint to give a one-element generating set with norm symmetry. Type I Optimal Normal Bases remove Gram matrix inversion scaling; towered Galois evaluations reduce key-switching noise; Hensel's and Nakayama's lemmas extend the mapping to Galois rings for integer arithmetic. Extending to boundless dimensions via ciphertext-level Strassen with deferred trace drops multi-ciphertext basis switching entirely. Canonical norm arguments establish power bases as optimal for the non-evaluated outer tensors. The authors declare no competing interests and note that no datasets were generated or analyzed.

Algorithms

The paper contains three formal algorithms. All three are reproduced in full below, followed by the towered trace procedure, which the body specifies as a theorem (Theorem 14) and invokes procedurally as ToweredTrace in Algorithm 3 rather than as a numbered listing.

Algorithm 1: Constructive Normal Basis Embedding

Input:  Matrices A in M_{r x s}(F_p), B in M_{s x t}(F_p),
        bases {u_i}_{i=0}^{r-1} of R1, {w_k}_{k=0}^{t-1} of R3,
        generators theta, gamma_0 in R2
Output: Polynomial encodings P_A, P_B in R1 (x) R2 (x) R3  ~=  R

 1: Initialize P_A = 0, P_B = 0
 2: for i = 0, ..., r-1 do
 3:     for j = 0, ..., s-1 do
 4:         P_A <- P_A + a_ij * ( u_i (x) sigma^j(theta) (x) 1 )
 5:     end for
 6: end for
 7: for j = 0, ..., s-1 do
 8:     for k = 0, ..., t-1 do
 9:         P_B <- P_B + b_jk * ( 1 (x) sigma^j(gamma_0) (x) w_k )
10:     end for
11: end for
12: return P_A, P_B

Under an SDNB, $\gamma_0 = \theta$, so lines 4 and 9 use the same single generator. Here $\sigma$ is the Frobenius automorphism of $\overline{R}_2 / \mathbb{F}_p$ and $(x)$ denotes the tensor product.

Algorithm 2: Logarithmic Trace Evaluation Sequence

Input:  Ciphertext c = (c_0, c_1) in R_q^2, degree s, base B, bound D,
        evaluation keys evk
Output: Trace ciphertext c_out = sum_{i=0}^{s-1} sigma^i(c)

 1: Decompose the initial masking polynomial c_1 = sum_{m=0}^{D} c_1^{(m)} B^m
 2: function TraceSum(c, {c_1^{(m)}}, k)
 3:     if k = 1 then return c
 4:     end if
 5:     h <- floor(k / 2)
 6:     c_half <- TraceSum(c, {c_1^{(m)}}, h)
 7:     if h = 1 then
 8:         {d_1^{(m)}} <- {c_1^{(m)}}
 9:     else
10:         Decompose the masking polynomial c_half,1 = sum_{m=0}^{D} d_1^{(m)} B^m
11:     end if
12:     c_eval <- ( sigma^h(c_half,0), 0 )
                  + sum_{m=0}^{D} sigma^h(d_1^{(m)}) * evk_{sigma^h}^{(m)}
13:     c_next <- c_half + c_eval
14:     if k = 1 (mod 2) then
15:         c_rem <- ( sigma^{k-1}(c_0), 0 )
                      + sum_{m=0}^{D} sigma^{k-1}(c_1^{(m)}) * evk_{sigma^{k-1}}^{(m)}
16:         c_next <- c_next + c_rem
17:     end if
18:     return c_next
19: end function
20: c_out <- TraceSum(c, {c_1^{(m)}}, s)
21: return c_out

Cost (Theorem 13): $\lfloor \log_2 s \rfloor$ digit-extraction applications and $\lfloor \log_2 s \rfloor + \mathrm{hw}(s) - 1$ key switches. Under the SDNB configuration the recursion is run with $s'= s/2$ terms instead of $s$.

Algorithm 3: Boundless Homomorphic Block-Strassen Evaluation

Input:  Matrices A, B in M_N(F_p), base module capacity N_max
Output: Encrypted block matrix {c_C^{(i,j)}}_{i,j=1}^{L} encrypting C = A B

 1: L <- 2^ceil(log2(N / N_max))
 2: Pad A and B to (L * N_max) x (L * N_max) with zeros and partition
    into L x L blocks {A^(i,j)}, {B^(i,j)}

    Phase 1: Offline client encoding and encryption
 3: for i = 1..L, j = 1..L do
 4:     P_A^(i,j) <- NormalBasisEncode(A^(i,j), primary generator theta)
 5:     P_B^(i,j) <- NormalBasisEncode(B^(i,j), trace dual generator gamma_0)
 6:     c_A^(i,j) <- BGV.Enc_pk(P_A^(i,j));   c_B^(i,j) <- BGV.Enc_pk(P_B^(i,j))
 7: end for

    Phase 2: Homomorphic Strassen tensor products (server)
 8: function HomoStrassen(X, Y, k)
 9:     if k = 1 then
10:         return EvalMult_relin(X^(1,1), Y^(1,1))
11:     end if
12:     Split X into quadrants X11, X12, X21, X22 and Y into Y11, Y12, Y21, Y22
13:     M1 <- HomoStrassen(X11 + X22, Y11 + Y22, k/2)
14:     M2 <- HomoStrassen(X21 + X22, Y11,       k/2)
15:     M3 <- HomoStrassen(X11,       Y12 - Y22, k/2)
16:     M4 <- HomoStrassen(X22,       Y21 - Y11, k/2)
17:     M5 <- HomoStrassen(X11 + X12, Y22,       k/2)
18:     M6 <- HomoStrassen(X21 - X11, Y11 + Y12, k/2)
19:     M7 <- HomoStrassen(X12 - X22, Y21 + Y22, k/2)
20:     C11 <- M1 + M4 - M5 + M7
21:     C12 <- M3 + M5
22:     C21 <- M2 + M4
23:     C22 <- M1 - M2 + M3 + M6
24:     return Recombine quadrants C11, C12, C21, C22 into k x k block grid C
25: end function
26: M_tensor <- HomoStrassen(c_A, c_B, L)

    Phase 3: Deferred trace extraction (server)
27: for i = 1..L, j = 1..L do
28:     c_C^(i,j) <- ToweredTrace(M_tensor^(i,j))
29: end for
30: return {c_C^(i,j)}

The trace in Phase 3 runs after the Strassen recombination, exploiting $\mathrm{Tr}(\sum_k M_k) = \sum_k \mathrm{Tr}(M_k)$, so only $L^2$ trace evaluations are needed instead of $O(L^{\log_2 7})$, and no homomorphic basis switching occurs anywhere (CMult $= 0$).

Towered trace procedure (Theorem 14)

Input:  Ciphertext c in R_q^2 with composite trace degree s = d_1 * d_2,
        moduli q > q', evaluation keys evk
Output: Ciphertext encrypting Tr_{F_{p^s} / F_p}(m)

 1: # Inner trace over Gal(F_{p^s} / F_{p^{d_1}}), generated by sigma^{d_1}
 2: c_inner <- TraceSum over the d_2 automorphisms {sigma^{d_1 * i}}_{i=0}^{d_2 - 1}
              using the logarithmic recursion of Algorithm 2
    # accumulated key-switching error: O(d_2) * B_ks
 3: # Intermediate modulus switch absorbs that error into rounding noise
 4: c_scaled <- BGV.ModSwitch(c_inner, q -> q')
 5: # Outer trace over Gal(F_{p^{d_1}} / F_p), generated by sigma
 6: c_out <- TraceSum over the d_1 automorphisms {sigma^i}_{i=0}^{d_1 - 1}
            applied to c_scaled
    # fresh independent key-switching error: O(d_1) * B_ks
 7: return c_out

Total additive key-switching noise is $O(d_1 + d_2) B_{\mathrm{ks}}$ instead of $O(s) B_{\mathrm{ks}}$; recursing on a fully factored $s$ gives $O(\log s)$. In the reported experiments the towered trace executes 5 sequential additions and 5 key-switching automorphisms.
